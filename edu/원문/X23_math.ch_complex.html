X23. Complex Analysis and Transforms, for People Who Build Them

Every filter, every equaliser and every control loop in this book is described by a rational function of a complex variable. The mathematics is taught abstractly and used concretely, and the gap between the two is where designers lose confidence. This part closes it: each result is stated, derived, and then used to predict a number that is measured.

X23.1 Why the z-transform is the right tool

From a difference equation to a rational function
StepJustification
1A linear time-invariant system obeys y[n] = Σbkx[n−k] − Σaky[n−k].The general recursive filter. This is also literally the RTL: each delayed term is a register.
2Define X(z) = Σx[n]z−n.The z-transform. The only property we need is the next line.
3A delay by one sample multiplies the transform by z−1.Substitute and shift the summation index. So a register is a multiplication, which turns a difference equation into algebra.
4Therefore H(z) = Y/X = (Σbkz−k) / (1 + Σakz−k).Divide. The transfer function is a ratio of polynomials.
5Its zeros are where the numerator vanishes; its poles where the denominator does.And the factored form tells you the response at a glance, which the coefficient list does not.
6Evaluating on z = ejω gives the frequency response; a zero near the circle makes a notch, a pole near it makes a peak.Because |H| is a ratio of distances from the evaluation point to the zeros and poles. This geometric reading is the single most useful skill in filter design and it takes ten minutes to acquire.

The last two columns are an independent cross-check: the textbook approximation says the bandwidth is about 2(1−r) radians, and the measurement confirms it as r → 1 and departs from it when the pole is far from the circle, exactly as the approximation's derivation requires.
Why this matters in fixed point, which is where the theory earns its keep. Quantising the coefficients moves the poles. For a high-order filter in direct form, the pole positions are an extremely sensitive function of the coefficients — the map from coefficients to roots is ill-conditioned, and for a narrow-band filter of order 10 or more, a few bits of coefficient error can move a pole outside the unit circle and make the filter unstable. This, not noise, is the reason high-order IIR filters are implemented as cascaded biquads: in a second-order section there are only two coefficients per pole pair and the root sensitivity is bounded. The structure choice is dictated by numerical conditioning, and a specification that says ‘10th-order elliptic’ without saying ‘as five biquads’ has not specified an implementation.

The delay row is the one worth remembering. A pure delay is transcendental in s and trivial in z. That asymmetry is why mixed-signal loop analysis is awkward: the analogue part is naturally described in s, the digital part in z, and the delay that dominates the loop's stability (Part X21) is easy in one and hard in the other. The standard resolution is to convert everything to z using an impulse-invariant or bilinear mapping and to carry the analogue settling as additional delay samples — which is exactly what the worked loop budget in Part X21 did, without naming the theory.

X23.3 Residues, partial fractions and why they are the impulse response

Partial fractions give the impulse response by inspection
StepJustification
1Factor H(z) = Σk Ak/(1 − pkz−1) for distinct poles.Partial fraction expansion; Ak is the residue at the pole.
2Each term inverse-transforms to Akpknu[n].The geometric sequence is the transform pair everything rests on.
3So h[n] = ΣAkpkn — a sum of decaying (or growing) exponentials.Every IIR impulse response is exactly this. Nothing else can happen.
4|pk| < 1 gives decay; |pk| > 1 gives growth.Which is the stability criterion, now derived rather than asserted.
5The time constant of a pole at radius r is −1/ln r samples.The practical form: a pole at 0.99 settles in about 100 samples, one at 0.999 in about 1000. Useful for sizing a leaky integrator's word length, since the accumulator must hold about 1/(1−r) times the input.

Problem. Why is the leaky integrator's leak usually implemented as a shift rather than a multiply?
Solution. Because choosing r = 1 − 2−m makes the update y ← y − (y >> m) + x, which is a shift and two adds and no multiplier — and at the rates in Part X4 a multiplier in a per-symbol loop is simply not available. The costs are worth naming. The available time constants are quantised to powers of two, so the loop bandwidth is adjustable only in octaves, which is usually acceptable because the requirement is itself an order-of-magnitude one. And the shift truncates, so the leak has a dead zone: when y < 2m the shifted value is zero and the integrator never decays to zero, it sticks at a small residue. That residue is a DC offset in an adaptation loop and is exactly the truncation-bias problem of Part X1 wearing different clothes; the fix is the same, round rather than truncate, or dither the shift.
Problem. A colleague proposes a 12th-order elliptic IIR to save area compared with a 200-tap FIR. What do you check before agreeing?
Solution. Four things, in order of how likely they are to kill it. Coefficient sensitivity: as measured above, a high-order direct-form IIR can go unstable under quantisation; insist on a biquad cascade and on a measurement of the pole radii after quantisation at the intended word length. Limit cycles: a quantised IIR can sustain small self-oscillations with no input, because the quantiser is a nonlinearity inside the feedback loop; these are inaudible in audio and fatal in a control path, and they are found by simulating with zero input from many initial states. Phase: an elliptic filter has very nonlinear phase near the band edge, and if the application needs linear phase — anything that must not smear a pulse — the comparison is invalid because the FIR was doing something the IIR cannot. Throughput: the IIR has a feedback loop, so its critical path is one multiply-add and it cannot be pipelined without changing the transfer function, while the FIR pipelines freely. At high sample rates this alone decides it. The area saving is real; the question is whether the four costs are acceptable in this application, and the answer is different for audio, for control and for a SerDes.
