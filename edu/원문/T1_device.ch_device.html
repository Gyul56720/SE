T1. The MOSFET as Four Different Machines

One device, four operating regions — and you design a different circuit in each.

A MOSFET is usually introduced as a switch, and for a first digital course that is enough. It is not enough to design production IP. The same four-terminal device behaves as four qualitatively different machines depending on the voltages you put on it, and a working designer moves between all four — often inside one block. A sampling front end uses the device as a resistor; the comparator behind it uses the device as an amplifier; the digital logic behind that uses it as a switch; and the leakage that drains the battery while all of it sits idle is the device acting as a very bad switch in the region nobody designed for.

This chapter establishes the four regions, then — for each — states where you use it, when you reach for it, how you apply it, and what the code that ships with a real IP deliverable looks like. Nothing here is included unless it is used later in this book.

T1.1 What the gate actually does

Before the regions, one physical fact, because every later equation rests on it. The gate is one plate of a capacitor. The channel — the thin sheet of silicon under the oxide — is the other. Between them sits an insulator a few atoms thick.

Gate oxide capacitance per unit area, Cox — εox/tox. With a physical oxide thickness of 1.2 nm and εox ≈ 3.9 ε0, this is about 16 mF/m² — roughly 1.60×1019 fF per square micrometre. Everything the transistor does is proportional to it.
유도 — Why gate voltage controls current at all
이렇게 된다왜냐하면
1Put a positive voltage on the gate of an NMOS. The gate is one plate of a capacitor, so it stores charge Q = Cox·V per unit area.That is the definition of capacitance. No transistor physics yet.
2The opposite charge must appear on the other plate — in the silicon, directly under the oxide.A capacitor holds equal and opposite charge on its plates.
3In p-type silicon the mobile negative charge is electrons, which are scarce. Below some gate voltage the silicon supplies the required charge by pushing holes away instead, leaving fixed negative acceptor ions.Depleting the majority carriers is cheaper than creating minority carriers.
4Above some gate voltage the depletion stops growing and the silicon starts supplying mobile electrons instead. That sheet of electrons is the channel.Once the surface is pulled far enough, attracting electrons costs less energy than widening the depletion region.
5That crossover gate voltage is the threshold voltage Vt.It is not a switch trip point designed into the device; it is the voltage at which the silicon changes which kind of charge it supplies.
6Above Vt, the mobile charge in the channel is Qch = Cox(VGS − Vt) per unit area.The first Vt worth of gate voltage went into depletion; only the excess produces mobile charge.
7Current is charge times velocity, and velocity is mobility times field: I = W·Qch·μ·E.Drift current, the same relation that gives Ohm's law in a resistor.

That last line is the whole device. Every equation below is this relation with a different assumption about how the field E varies along the channel. The quantity VGS − Vt appears so often it has a name: the overdrive, written Vov. When a designer says “I ran that device at 150 millivolts of overdrive”, this is the number.

Vt is not one number. It moves with temperature (roughly −1 mV/°C), with body bias, with channel length, with how close the neighbouring polysilicon is, and it differs from die to die and from transistor to transistor on the same die. A design that works only for the nominal Vt does not work. Chapter T27 makes this quantitative; for now, treat every Vt in this chapter as the centre of a distribution.

T1.2 Region 1 — cutoff: the switch that does not quite switch off

Cutoff and subthreshold leakage

Below threshold the channel is not empty — it is exponentially sparse. The current does not stop; it falls by a factor of ten for every 85 mV that VGS drops below Vt. That slope is the subthreshold swing, S, and it is the single most important number in a low-power design.

Ileak = I0 · 10(VGS − Vt)/S

S has a floor. The channel charge responds to the surface potential through a Boltzmann factor, so S ≥ (kT/q)·ln10 = 59.5 mV/decade at room temperature, and real devices land 20–40 % above that because part of the gate voltage is lost to the depletion capacitance. This floor is why leakage became the dominant power term as supplies scaled: you cannot lower Vt to keep speed without paying exponentially in leakage, and you cannot make the exponent steeper.

어디에 쓰나Standby power of every block you ship. Retention registers. Memory arrays (where leakage is multiplied by millions of cells). Power gating and the sizing of the header/footer switch. Any IP sold with a quoted standby current — which is most of them.
언제 쓰나Whenever you write a leakage number into a datasheet; whenever you choose between device flavours in synthesis; whenever a customer asks for a retention mode. Also whenever a block is mostly idle — an equaliser that runs for 3 % of the time is a leakage problem, not a dynamic-power problem.
어떻게 쓰나Get Ioff per micrometre of width from the foundry's characterisation at the worst corner (highest temperature, lowest Vt, highest supply — leakage worsens in the opposite direction from speed). Multiply by the total width in the block. Multiply again by the temperature factor — a rule of thumb is 2× per 10 °C, which follows directly from the S equation because S itself is proportional to T. Then decide: if the answer exceeds budget, move the non-critical paths to high-Vt cells and re-time.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
// Liberty (.lib) — what the foundry actually ships you.
// This is the leakage the synthesiser reads. Note that it is
// state-dependent: a NAND2 leaks differently per input pattern,
// because the stacked device changes the internal node voltage.

cell (NAND2_X1) {
    area : 1.596 ;
    cell_leakage_power : 4.2851e+03 ;        // nW, the default
    leakage_power () {
        when            : "!A1 & !A2" ;
        value           : 1.8032e+03 ;       // both off: stack effect helps
    }
    leakage_power () {
        when            : "A1 & !A2" ;
        value           : 4.9210e+03 ;
    }
    leakage_power () {
        when            : "A1 & A2" ;
        value           : 6.1104e+03 ;       // both on: worst
    }
    ...
}

# Synthesis — trade leakage against timing by device flavour.
# The tool will use LVT only where it must to close timing.

여기서 틀린다. Quoting leakage at nominal temperature. Leakage roughly doubles per 10 °C, so a 25 °C number is 8–16× optimistic against a 125 °C automotive corner. A datasheet that says “standby 12 µA” without naming the temperature, supply and corner is not a specification — and a customer who discovers the real number at their thermal limit will not buy from you again.
예제 1. Leakage of a block, done the way a datasheet requires
주어진 것. A block with 40,000 gates, average device width 0.8 µm per gate (both n and p), Ioff = 0.01 nA/µm at VGS = 0, 25 °C, typical corner. The customer runs it at 105 °C and asks for the worst-case standby current.
푸는 법. Total width, times Ioff per micrometre, times the temperature factor (2× per 10 °C), times the corner factor for fast-fast silicon (typically 2–3× on leakage).
답. Width = 40,000 × 0.8 = 32,000 µm. At 25 °C, typical: 32,000 × 0.01 nA = 0.24 µA. Temperature: 105 − 25 = 80 °C, so 28 = 256×. Corner: ×2.5. Total ≈ 0.16 mA.
The factor-of-2-per-10 °C rule is an approximation of the exponential; for a real deliverable you read the number off the foundry's corner tables rather than extrapolating. The point of the estimate is to know, early, whether you are within a factor of two of the budget or a factor of fifty.
여기서 틀린다. The 25 °C typical number and the 105 °C fast number differ by 640× here. Engineers quote the first because it is the one the tool prints by default. The second is the one the customer measures. Always state the triple — temperature, supply, process corner — next to the number; a leakage figure without it is meaningless.

T1.3 Region 2 — triode: the transistor as a resistor

Triode (linear) region and on-resistance

Turn the gate well above Vt and hold the drain close to the source. Now the channel exists along its whole length and the field along it is nearly uniform, so the device behaves like a resistor whose value the gate controls:

ID = μCox(W/L)[(VGS − Vt)VDS − VDS²/2]

For small VDS the square term vanishes and the device is simply

Ron = 1 / [μCox(W/L)(VGS − Vt)]

Read that denominator. Ron is inversely proportional to overdrive. If the signal you are passing moves VS, then VGS moves with it, and the resistance changes with the signal. A signal-dependent resistance in a sampling path is distortion, and it is the reason bootstrapped switches exist.

어디에 쓰나Every transmission gate and pass gate. The sampling switch at the front of every ADC and every track-and-hold. Power-gating headers and footers. The programmable resistors in a CTLE. Write drivers in SRAM. Any place a digital signal must be routed rather than regenerated.
언제 쓰나When you need to connect two nodes rather than drive one. The moment you catch yourself writing “pass the signal through” rather than “buffer the signal”, you are in triode and Ron matters.
어떻게 쓰나Budget the settling first, then size from it. A switch with resistance R charging capacitance C settles with time constant τ = RC; to settle to N bits you need tsettle ≥ RC·ln(2N+1). Invert for R, then use Ron = 1/[μCox(W/L)Vov] to get W/L. Then check Ron at the worst signal level, not at mid-scale: for an NMOS passing a rising signal, VGS shrinks as the signal rises and Ron blows up near VDD − Vt.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
* SPICE — the measurement you actually run on a sampling switch.
* You are not checking that it works; you are checking how Ron varies
* with the signal, because that variation is the distortion.

.param vin_val=0.45
M1  drain gate source bulk nch  W=2u  L=30n
Vg  gate  0  DC 0.9
Vs  source 0 DC 'vin_val'
Vd  drain  0 DC 'vin_val+1m'      $ 1 mV across the switch: stay in triode
.dc vin_val 0 0.9 0.01
.measure dc ron  PARAM='1m/I(Vd)'  $ R = V/I at each input level
.alter
.param boot=1                      $ repeat with a bootstrapped gate
.end

// Verilog-AMS behavioural model used in mixed-signal top-level sims.
// A digital verification engineer meets this, not the SPICE deck.
module sw_nmos (inout d, inout s, input g);
    parameter real ron_nom = 250.0;     // ohms at mid-scale
    parameter real roff    = 1e12;
    analog begin
        // Ron rises as (Vgs - Vt) falls: this is the term that matters.
        real vgs, ron;
        vgs = V(g) - V(s);
        ron = (vgs > 0.35) ? ron_nom * 0.55 / (vgs - 0.35) : roff;
        I(d,s) <+ V(d,s) / ron;
    end
endmodule
여기서 틀린다. Sizing the switch at mid-scale and declaring it done. Ron at the top of the input range can be several times its mid-scale value, and the settling error it causes is signal-dependent — which means it is harmonic distortion, not noise, and averaging will not remove it. Sweep Ron across the full input range and size for the worst point.
예제 2. Sizing a sampling switch from the settling requirement
주어진 것. A 10-bit converter samples onto C = 1 pF in 2 ns. Supply 0.9 V, Vt = 0.3 V, and the worst-case input is 0.5 V, so Vov = 0.9 − 0.5 − 0.35 = 0.1 V.
푸는 법. Settling to 10 bits needs the error below ½ LSB, i.e. e−t/τ < 2−11, so t/τ > 11·ln2 = 7.62. Use a 2× margin on τ for the rest of the path, then solve R from τ = RC, then W/L from Ron.
답. τ ≤ 2 ns / 7.62 = 262 ps. With 2× margin, τ = 131 ps. R = τ/C = 131 Ω. Then W/L = 1/(μCox·R·Vov) = 317.
여기서 틀린다. Using Vov at the mid-scale input (0.45 V) instead of the worst-case input gives Vov = 0.1 V and a W/L about 2× smaller — and then the switch misses its settling target exactly where the signal is largest, which is where the distortion is most visible. Size switches at the worst signal level, not the average one.

T1.4 Region 3 — saturation: the transistor as a current source

Saturation, transconductance, and output resistance

Raise VDS above the overdrive and the channel pinches off near the drain: the local VGS there falls to Vt and no more mobile charge exists. Beyond that point, raising the drain voltage no longer raises the current much. The device has become a current source controlled by the gate:

ID = ½ μCox(W/L)(VGS − Vt)² · (1 + λVDS)

Two derived quantities do all the work in analog design:

Transconductance gm = ∂ID/∂VGS = 2ID/Vov. How much current you get per volt of input — i.e. the gain-producing term.

Output resistance ro = ∂VDS/∂ID ≈ 1/(λID). How close to an ideal current source it is. The term λ exists because pinch-off moves with drain voltage — channel-length modulation — and it is worse for short channels, which is why analog blocks use devices several times the minimum length.

Their product gmro = 2/(λVov) is the intrinsic gain: the most voltage gain one transistor can give you. It falls as processes scale, which is why modern analog design leans on digital assistance rather than on raw gain.

어디에 쓰나Comparators and sense amplifiers — the pre-amp stage. Every current mirror and bias network. CTLE and the analogue front end of a SerDes. Level shifters during the transition. Ring-oscillator delay cells in a PLL. Anywhere the circuit must amplify rather than switch.
언제 쓰나When the question is “how much gain” or “how much current, independent of the load”. Also, unexpectedly, in digital analysis: during a logic transition both devices pass through saturation, and the short-circuit current that flows then is the term T4 uses to explain why slow input edges cost power.
어떻게 쓰나Design with gm/ID rather than with W/L. Pick the gm you need from the gain or the noise requirement; pick gm/ID from the speed/power trade (high gm/ID ≈ low overdrive ≈ efficient but slow; low gm/ID ≈ high overdrive ≈ fast but power-hungry); that fixes ID; read W/L off the foundry's characterised gm/ID curves. This works across processes and does not rely on the square-law equation, which stops being accurate below about 100 nm.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
* The gm/ID sweep every analog team runs once per process,
* then reuses for every block. You are not simulating a circuit here;
* you are characterising the device so design becomes look-up.

.param L_val=100n
M1  d g 0 0 nch  W=10u  L='L_val'
Vg  g 0 DC 0.5
Vd  d 0 DC 0.45          $ hold Vds at VDD/2, in saturation
.dc Vg 0.2 0.9 0.005
.measure dc gm_id  PARAM='deriv(I(Vd),V(g))/I(Vd)'
.measure dc ft     PARAM='deriv(I(Vd),V(g))/(2*3.14159*cgg)'
.step param L_val list 30n 60n 100n 200n 500n
.end

// What a digital designer sees of all this: a characterised cell.
// The analog block arrives as a .lib timing/power view plus a
// behavioural model. You must still know what is inside, because
// the constraints you write depend on it.
cell (CMP_STRONGARM) {
    pin (CLK)  { direction : input;  clock : true;  capacitance : 4.8; }
    pin (VINP) { direction : input;  capacitance : 12.1; }
    pin (OUTP) { direction : output;
                 timing () {
                     related_pin  : "CLK" ;
                     timing_type  : rising_edge ;
                     cell_rise (delay_template) { ... }
                 } }
    // The number a digital designer must not ignore:
    // the comparator has a metastability window like any bistable.
    // T14 derives it; the vendor states it here.
}
여기서 틀린다. Using the square-law equation for design in a modern process. Below roughly 100 nm the velocity of carriers saturates, the exponent drifts from 2 toward 1, and the equation can be wrong by tens of percent. It remains excellent for reasoning — it tells you the right direction and the right sensitivity — and useless for numbers. Reason with the equation, size from the characterised curves.

T1.5 Region 4 — subthreshold as a design space, not only as leakage

Subthreshold operation

The same exponential that produces leakage is a usable transfer characteristic. Biased below Vt, the device gives gm/ID at its maximum — about 27.1 V⁻¹, set only by S — which is the best current efficiency any MOSFET can offer. The cost is speed: fT falls by orders of magnitude, and the exponential dependence on Vt means that device-to-device Vt mismatch turns into current mismatch of tens of percent.

So subthreshold is the region you choose when energy per operation dominates and speed does not, and the region you are forced into when a supply droops. It is also the region a retention circuit lives in: state is held by a device that is barely on.

어디에 쓰나Always-on domains — wake-up logic, watchdog timers, retention registers. Sensor front ends. Bias generators that must survive at very low current. And, negatively, every leakage path in every block you ship.
언제 쓰나When the duty cycle is very low and the energy budget is per-operation rather than per-second. When a customer asks for a retention mode with a quoted microamp figure. When you are asked to survive a supply that collapses toward Vt.
어떻게 쓰나Accept that matching, not speed, will set your area. In strong inversion, current mismatch tracks Vt mismatch through gm/ID; in subthreshold gm/ID is at its maximum, so the same Vt mismatch produces the largest current mismatch. Size the devices from the matching requirement (T28's Pelgrom relation), then check speed, rather than the other way round.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
# UPF (Unified Power Format) — how the always-on domain is
# declared to the tools. A digital IP deliverable that supports
# retention ships this file, and it is as much a part of the
# product as the RTL.

create_power_domain PD_ALWAYS_ON -elements {u_wake u_timer}
create_power_domain PD_CORE      -elements {u_dsp u_eq}   -scope .

create_supply_net  VDD_AON  -domain PD_ALWAYS_ON
create_supply_net  VDD_CORE -domain PD_CORE
set_domain_supply_net PD_CORE -primary_power_net VDD_CORE \
                              -primary_ground_net VSS

# Retention: state survives while VDD_CORE collapses.
set_retention  RET_CORE -domain PD_CORE \
    -retention_power_net VDD_AON -retention_ground_net VSS \
    -save_signal  {u_dsp/ret_save  high} \
    -restore_signal {u_dsp/ret_restore low}

# Isolation: outputs of a collapsed domain must not float into
# the always-on domain, or the always-on logic burns crowbar current.
set_isolation ISO_CORE -domain PD_CORE \
    -isolation_power_net VDD_AON -clamp_value 0 \
    -applies_to outputs
set_isolation_control ISO_CORE -domain PD_CORE \
    -isolation_signal u_pmc/iso_en -isolation_sense high
여기서 틀린다. Forgetting isolation. When a power domain collapses, its outputs drift to an intermediate voltage. Feed that into an always-on gate and both its devices conduct — the always-on domain then draws milliamps instead of microamps, and the retention mode you sold does not exist. This is one of the most common integration failures in low-power IP, and it is invisible in RTL simulation because RTL has no intermediate voltage.

T1.6 Two second-order effects you cannot ignore

Body effect

Vt is quoted for source tied to bulk. Lift the source above the bulk and the depletion region under the channel widens, so more gate voltage is needed before inversion: Vt rises. The dependence is Vt = Vt0 + γ(√(2φF + VSB) − √(2φF)), and γ is a process constant of order 0.2–0.5 √V.

The practical consequence: a device whose source is not at the rail is weaker than the library says.

어디에 쓰나Stacked devices in NAND/NOR — the upper NMOS has its source above ground. Pass-gate chains. Cascode current sources. Multiplexer trees built from transmission gates. Any place transistors are in series.
언제 쓰나Whenever you stack. A 4-input NAND is not simply 4× slower than an inverter — it is worse, because the upper devices are body-effected. This is one reason wide gates are decomposed into trees rather than built flat.
어떻게 쓰나For hand analysis, add the Vt shift and recompute the overdrive of the upper device. For real sizing, let the characterised library handle it — the .lib delay tables already contain the effect — but know it is there so that a stacked structure you invent is not assumed to scale linearly. If the stack is deep, insert a buffer instead.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
// Why you will not find `body effect` in an RTL file, but will
// feel it: the synthesiser's cell choice reflects it.
// These two are logically identical and time very differently.

assign y = ~(a & b & c & d);          // maps to NAND4 — deep stack

wire ab = ~(a & b);                   // maps to NAND2 + NAND2 + NOR2
wire cd = ~(c & d);                   // shallower stacks, more cells
assign y = ~(~ab | ~cd) ? 1'b0 : 1'b1;

// Liberty shows the penalty directly. Compare the intrinsic delays:
//   NAND2_X1  cell_rise (A1) ~ 0.021 ns
//   NAND3_X1  cell_rise (A1) ~ 0.031 ns
//   NAND4_X1  cell_rise (A1) ~ 0.045 ns     <- worse than linear
여기서 틀린다. Assuming a stack of n devices is n times slower. Body effect and the internal node capacitances make it super-linear, and above three or four the penalty grows fast. If a synthesised netlist contains NAND4s on a critical path, that is usually the first thing to restructure.
Gate capacitance — the load you are driving

The gate you drive is a capacitor: Cg ≈ Cox·W·L, plus overlap capacitance that does not scale with L. For 1.2 nm oxide this is of order 1.60×107 fF/µm² of gate area.

This single fact ties the whole of digital design together: the output of one gate must charge the input capacitance of the next, so delay is proportional to the ratio of load capacitance to drive strength. That ratio is the electrical effort of T3, and it is why sizing is a chain problem rather than a per-gate problem.

There is also a term that bites in analog and in high-speed digital: the gate-to-drain overlap capacitance Cgd appears at the output multiplied by the gain of the stage (Miller effect). In a comparator pre-amp this can dominate the load; in a digital gate it is why the input capacitance seen during a transition is larger than the static value.

어디에 쓰나Every delay calculation. Clock-tree power (the clock drives enormous total gate capacitance, which is why clock gating is the first power optimisation). Input capacitance you quote in a datasheet so a customer can budget their driver.
언제 쓰나Always, in digital. Specifically when you write the input capacitance line of an IP datasheet, and when you decide how many loads one cell may drive.
어떻게 쓰나Express loads in units of a minimum inverter's input capacitance and the arithmetic becomes tractable by hand — this is exactly what logical effort (T3) formalises. For the deliverable, state input capacitance per pin in the .lib and in the datasheet; integrators use it to size their drivers and to build the clock tree.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
// The datasheet line that comes from this, and the .lib it matches.
// If these two disagree, the customer's timing closure fails and
// they will find out before you do.

pin (D) {
    direction          : input ;
    capacitance        : 1.412 ;       // fF — what the integrator budgets
    rise_capacitance   : 1.455 ;
    fall_capacitance   : 1.369 ;
    max_transition     : 0.300 ;       // ns — beyond this, delay tables
}                                      //      are extrapolated, not valid

// SDC the customer writes against your block:
set_load       0.0014   [get_ports eq_out*]    ;# pF
set_driving_cell -lib_cell BUF_X4 [get_ports adc_data*]
set_max_transition 0.300 [current_design]
여기서 틀린다. Letting a net exceed max_transition. Beyond that point the library's delay tables are being extrapolated, and the timing report is no longer characterised data — it is a guess with a confident number next to it. Treat a max-transition violation as a hard error, not a warning.

A digital cell spends almost all of its time in the first two rows and passes briefly through the third on every transition. That brief passage is not a detail: it is the short-circuit current of T4 and the regeneration of T13, and two of the hardest problems in this book — metastability and comparator resolution — are entirely arguments about what happens while a device sits in saturation instead of settling into triode or cutoff.

실제로 난 일
A production sampling switch was sized from its mid-scale Ron and met its settling target in simulation at every corner. Silicon showed second-harmonic distortion 12 dB worse than simulated. The cause was that the verification sweep held the input at mid-scale while checking settling, and swept the input only while checking gain — so the combination that mattered, large input and tight settling, was never simulated. Nothing in the design was wrong; the verification grid was wrong. T30 turns this into a rule: sweep the axes together, not one at a time.

특허로 가는 길 — 이 주제에서 발명이 일어나는 자리
Device-level invention is largely closed to a fabless IP house — the foundry owns the device. What is open is how you choose among the devices you are given. Claims in shipping IP portfolios routinely read on selection and arrangement: a retention latch that uses high-Vt devices only on the state-holding node while the read path stays standard-Vt; a level shifter whose pull-up stack is sized so that the contention current is bounded across corners; a sampling switch whose bootstrap network holds VGS constant so that Ron is signal-independent. All three are device-region arguments, and all three are patentable as circuits. Read this chapter asking which region am I in, and what does that buy me — that question is where the claims come from.
