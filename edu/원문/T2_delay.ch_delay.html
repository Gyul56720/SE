T2. Delay — From One RC to a Whole Timing Path

Every delay number a tool reports is one of four models. Know which one, or you will trust a number that was never about your circuit.

Ask four engineers what the delay of an inverter is and you get four answers, all correct, because they are answering with four different models. A SPICE user answers with a differential equation. A physical-design engineer answers with a two-dimensional lookup table from a Liberty file. A microarchitect answers in FO4. A synthesis engineer answers with a dimensionless number that has no picoseconds in it at all. These are not competing approximations of one truth; they are four tools with different jobs, and using the wrong one is how a design gets signed off on a number that was never about the path that failed.

This chapter builds them in order, each from the one before, and at every step states the regime in which that model is the right one to use. The numbers throughout come from one set of process constants declared at the top of this chapter's source — a 28 nm-class low-power node at 0.9 V — and every table below is computed from them, not typed in.

T2.1 The one model you can do in your head: switched RC

A CMOS gate charges and discharges a capacitor through a transistor that is acting as a resistor. That is the whole of it. Everything else in this chapter is a refinement of that sentence, and every refinement exists because a specific assumption in it broke.

RC지연 (RC delay) — A gate's output node is a capacitance C driven through an equivalent resistance R. The node's voltage is a decaying exponential, and the delay is the time to cross the receiver's switching threshold — conventionally 50 % of VDD.
유도 — From the transistor to 0.69 RC
이렇게 된다왜냐하면
1A switching gate connects the output node either to VDD through the PMOS stack or to ground through the NMOS stack.That is what a static CMOS gate is: two complementary networks, exactly one of which conducts in steady state.
2While it switches, the conducting transistor is not a resistor — it starts in saturation and ends in triode.VDS starts at VDD and falls toward 0, so the device crosses regions during the transition (T1).
3We replace it anyway with a single equivalent resistance Req, defined as the value that gives the same 50 % crossing time.This is a fitted quantity, not a measured resistance — which is why it differs between textbooks and why you never compare Req numbers across sources.
4Then V(t) = VDD·e−t/RC for a falling output.First-order linear ODE with a step excitation: the standard solution.
5Setting V = VDD/2 gives t = RC·ln 2 = 0.69 RC.ln 2 = 0.693. This is where the 0.69 in every delay formula comes from — it is the 50 % point, nothing more.
6For 10–90 % rise/fall time the same solution gives t = RC·ln 9 = 2.2 RC.Same exponential, different two crossings. 0.69 and 2.2 describe the same waveform — a delay and a slew are two readings of one curve.
τ = Req·Cg — the process's unit of time

Pick one reference device — the minimum-size inverter — and give it two numbers: an equivalent output resistance Req = 8 kΩ and an input capacitance Cg = 1 fF. Their product τ = 8 ps is the only absolute time in the rest of this chapter. Every other delay will be quoted as a multiple of it.

This is not a cosmetic choice. Ratios between gates are set by topology and are stable across process, voltage and temperature to within a few per cent; the absolute τ moves by 2× or more across corners. Separating the two is what lets you do architecture on paper and let the tool supply the picoseconds.

어디에 쓰나Every hand estimate of a critical path, every early architectural trade-off, every 'will this fit in one cycle' question before the RTL exists.
언제 쓰나Before synthesis — when there is no netlist to run STA on, and after STA, to check whether a reported number is even plausible.
어떻게 쓰나Express the path in τ, then multiply once at the end. A useful anchor is FO4 — an inverter driving four copies of itself — which in this process is 40 ps. A modern high-performance pipeline stage is roughly 12–20 FO4; a low-power one, 25–40.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
// A 'timing shell' exists so that the number you measure is about
// your logic, not about the pads. Synthesise the DUT inside registers:
module fir_shell (input clk, input rst, input signed [7:0] din, output signed [15:0] dout);
    reg signed  [7:0] din_q;
    reg signed [15:0] dout_q;
    wire signed [15:0] y;
    always @(posedge clk) begin din_q <= din; dout_q <= y; end
    fir4 u_dut (.clk(clk), .rst(rst), .x(din_q), .y(y));
    assign dout = dout_q;
endmodule
// Measured on this repository's own FIR: 455 MHz without the shell, 47 MHz with it.
// The 455 MHz was the tool timing a path that started and ended at a pin.
여기서 틀린다. Quoting Fmax from a design whose inputs and outputs are pins is the single most common false number in an IP datasheet. If a path has no launching and no capturing register, the tool has nothing to constrain and will report whatever combinational delay it found — often 10× optimistic.

T2.2 Elmore delay — when one RC becomes a chain of them

The single-RC model assumes the resistance is at one end and the capacitance at the other. A wire violates both: its resistance and its capacitance are distributed along it. Cutting it into N lumps and summing gives the Elmore delay, and the sum has a shape worth memorising, because it is the reason long wires do not scale.

유도 — Why wire delay grows with the square of length
이렇게 된다왜냐하면
1Cut a wire of length L into N equal sections, each with resistance R/N and capacitance C/N.R = r·L and C = c·L, with r in Ω/µm and c in F/µm from the technology file.
2The charge for every capacitance downstream of section i must flow through section i's resistance.Charge has only one path to reach them — that is the whole content of the Elmore model.
3So the delay is Σi (R/N)·(N−i+1)(C/N) = RC·(N+1)/(2N).Arithmetic series 1+2+…+N = N(N+1)/2. Substituting gives the closed form.
4As N → ∞ this tends to RC/2, and R and C are each proportional to L.Hence t ∝ r·c·L² — doubling a wire's length quadruples its delay, while a gate's delay does not change at all.
5For this process, a 1, µm wire gives RC/2 = 90 ps, and the four-lump approximation gives 112 ps.The coarse lump overestimates by 1.25×. Use the closed form for the distributed case and lumps only when the sections are real (a via, a branch, a tap).

Elmore delay

The first moment of the impulse response of an RC tree: for a node k, tk = Σi Rik·Ci, where Rik is the resistance shared by the path to node i and the path to node k. It is exact for a single RC, always an upper bound on the 50 % delay for an RC tree with a step input, and it ignores inductance entirely.

어디에 쓰나Clock-tree synthesis (balancing sinks), buffer and repeater insertion, and the delay model inside every placer's incremental timing engine — the placer cannot afford a full extraction per move.
언제 쓰나Whenever you need a delay that is monotone in the tree's structure and cheap to update. It is wrong by 10–30 % in absolute terms and right in its ordering, which is what an optimiser needs.
어떻게 쓰나Walk the tree from the driver; accumulate the shared resistance for each sink. Never use it to sign off timing — it is an optimisation objective, not a signoff model.

T2.3 Logical effort — delay with the picoseconds factored out

Sizing a chain of gates by RC arithmetic works and is miserable. Logical effort removes the process from the problem entirely, leaving a dimensionless expression you can optimise in your head, and it answers the two questions that actually come up: how many stages, and how much bigger should each one be.

유도 — The normalised delay of one stage
이렇게 된다왜냐하면
1Write a gate's delay as d = (Rgate·Cout) + (Rgate·Cself).The output node carries the load and the gate's own drain capacitance; both are charged through the same resistance.
2Divide through by τ = RinvCg.We choose to measure everything relative to the minimum inverter. Nothing is lost; the units are.
3The first term becomes g·h: logical effort g = how much worse this topology is than an inverter at driving current, and electrical effort h = Cout/Cin.g depends only on topology (NAND2 = 4/3, NOR2 = 5/3, inverter = 1); h depends only on the ratio of load to input, so it is independent of absolute size.
4The second term becomes p, the parasitic delay — the delay of the gate driving nothing.It scales with the gate's own diffusion, which grows with its width exactly as its drive does — so p is size-independent. pinv ≈ 1.
5d = g·h + p.Two multiplications and an addition, and no process constant anywhere.
6For a path of N stages, the total is minimised when every stage carries the same stage effort f = (G·B·H)1/N.Differentiate the sum with respect to each stage's size and set to zero; the symmetric solution falls out. G = Πg, H = Cload/Cin, and B accounts for branching off the path.
예제 5. Driving a 64× load: how many stages?
주어진 것. A 1× inverter must drive a load of 64·Cg (an on-chip bus with several receivers). Logical effort of an inverter chain is G = 1, no branching so B = 1, and H = 64, giving path effort F = 64.

어디에 쓰나Deciding how deep a pipeline stage can be before synthesis; choosing between a NAND-NAND and a NOR-NOR implementation; sizing a clock or reset distribution by hand; arguing with a synthesis result that looks wrong.
언제 쓰나At architecture time, and every time a tool's answer needs a sanity check. Synthesis does this internally but with a cost function that also weighs area and power, so its answer will differ — knowing the ideal tells you whether the difference is a trade or a mistake.
어떻게 쓰나Count G along the path, compute H from the real load, take F = G·B·H, then N ≈ log4F and f = F1/N. Work backwards from the load to size each stage: Cin,i = gi·Cout,i/f.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
# The synthesis constraint that makes the tool solve the same problem.
# Without a real load and a real driver, the tool optimises a fiction.
set_load        0.064            [get_ports dout*]   ;# pF -- from the receiver's .lib
set_driving_cell -lib_cell INVX4 [get_ports din*]    ;# not an ideal source
set_max_transition 0.150 [current_design]            ;# ns -- bounds slew degradation
set_max_fanout    16     [current_design]
# And the one that is almost always missing on a first pass:
set_max_capacitance 0.200 [current_design]
여기서 틀린다. g is a property of the topology, not of the cell you picked from the library. A library's NAND2_X4 and NAND2_X1 have the same g; only Cin differs. Engineers who 'fix timing' by swapping to a bigger cell without changing the stage count are changing h for the previous stage — they move the problem upstream, and on a long chain it comes back.

T2.4 Slew, and why a delay has two arguments

Both models so far take one input: the load. Every real timing tool takes two, because the delay of a gate depends on how fast its input moved. A slow input keeps both transistors partially on, wastes current through the stack, and starts the output late. That is the single largest correction to everything above, and it is why a Liberty file holds a table rather than a formula.

슬루 (slew / transition time) — The time for a signal to cross between two defined fractions of VDD, conventionally 10 %–90 % or 30 %–70 %. Which pair is a property of the library, and mixing libraries that use different pairs silently changes every delay in the design.
단락전류 (short-circuit current) — During a transition both the pull-up and pull-down networks conduct simultaneously, shorting VDD to ground through the stack. The charge lost this way is proportional to the input transition time; at normal slews it is 5–15 % of switching energy, and at degraded slews it can exceed the useful switching energy.

A real NLDM table is characterised by running SPICE at every grid point over the full corner set, typically 7×7 points per arc, per cell, per corner. What is real in the table is its shape: delay rises roughly linearly in load and roughly linearly in input slew, and the slew term is not small — at the light load it is the dominant term.
NLDM — the non-linear delay model, and what a tool actually reads

A Liberty (.lib) file gives, for each timing arc of each cell, two 2-D tables — one for delay and one for output slew — indexed by input transition time and total output capacitance. The tool bilinearly interpolates (and, at the edges, extrapolates) to get a number. Everything downstream — STA, synthesis cost, placement timing — is built on these tables.

Its successor, CCS (composite current source), stores a current waveform rather than a scalar, which matters when the receiver's input capacitance is itself voltage-dependent or when the net's resistance is large enough that the driver and the net interact. For an IP deliverable, state which model your numbers came from; they are not interchangeable at the 5 % level.

어디에 쓰나Every delay number in synthesis, STA, and place-and-route. Also in the IP datasheet, indirectly: your Fmax is only meaningful with the library and corner named.
언제 쓰나You read a .lib directly in exactly two situations: when a path's reported delay makes no sense and you want to see whether the tool extrapolated off the table, and when you are asked to support a library you have never seen.
어떻게 쓰나Find the arc, read index_1 (input slew) and index_2 (load), and check that your operating point is inside both. Off-table extrapolation is the standard cause of 'the tool says it passes and silicon says it does not'.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
/* Liberty, one arc of one cell -- this is what the tool reads. */
cell (INVX1) {
  area : 1.596 ;
  pin (A) { direction : input ;  capacitance : 1.02 ; }      /* fF */
  pin (Z) {
    direction : output ;
    function : "!A" ;
    max_capacitance : 64.0 ;
    timing () {
      related_pin : "A" ;
      timing_sense : negative_unate ;

        index_1 ("0.004, 0.012, 0.028, 0.060, 0.124, 0.252, 0.508");  /* input slew, ns */
        index_2 ("0.4, 1.6, 4.0, 8.8, 18.4, 37.6, 76.0");             /* load, fF */
        values ( "0.0091, 0.0142, 0.0241, 0.0431, 0.0812, 0.1574, 0.3098", \
                 "0.0113, 0.0164, 0.0263, 0.0453, 0.0834, 0.1596, 0.3120", \

여기서 틀린다. Delay and output slew are two separate tables, and the output slew is the next stage's input slew. This is how one badly driven net poisons a whole path: each stage receives a worse slew, produces a worse slew, and the degradation compounds. set_max_transition exists to stop that compounding, and a design with no max_transition constraint can meet setup in the report and fail in silicon.

T2.5 The wire, and the repeater that saves it

Gate delay shrinks with each process node. Wire delay does not: r rises as wires get thinner, c stays roughly constant per micron, and the die does not shrink as fast as the transistors. Beyond a length that has been getting shorter every node, a wire dominates the path, and the fix is not a bigger driver — it is to stop the wire being long.

유도 — Where the optimum comes from, and a check on it
이렇게 된다왜냐하면
1Cut the wire into k segments, each driven by an inverter of size h.One repeater per segment; the last one drives the receiver.
2Each segment costs 0.7·(Rinv/h)(Cw/k + h·Cg) for the driver, 0.4·RsCs for the distributed wire, and 0.7·Rs·hCg for the far-end load.The 0.4 and 0.7 are the standard distributed-RC coefficients — 0.4 for a wire driving its own capacitance, 0.69 for a lumped load.
3Sweeping k and h numerically gives 93.5 ps at k = 4, h = 38.Brute force over the whole grid — no assumption about the shape of the optimum.
4The closed forms k = √(0.4RwCw / 0.7RinvCg) and h = √(RinvCw / RwCg) give k = 3.59, h = 37.9.Independent cross-check. Two routes to the same answer, agreeing to 0.1 % in size. A single derivation that no one checked is how the wrong constant survives for years.
5Without repeaters, the same wire with its own best driver costs 131 ps.So repeaters buy 1.4× here — and that is the honest headline, not a figure taken from the most favourable corner of the sweep.
Repeater insertion

Break a wire whose delay is quadratic in length into segments short enough that the quadratic term is comparable to a buffer delay. The optimum segment length is the one where the wire's RC per segment equals the buffer's own delay; past that, you are paying buffers to save nothing.

For this process the crossover is around a wire of 25 µm — shorter than that and a repeater costs more than it saves.

어디에 쓰나Any net that leaves a block: block-to-block datapaths, clock spines, reset distribution, and the top-level assembly of an SoC where your IP's ports land.
언제 쓰나Decided in physical design, but constrained in your deliverable: if your IP's output has 200 ps of budget and the integrator's wire needs 300, the block fails through no fault of its RTL.
어떻게 쓰나Give the integrator a number, not a hope: state output delay, drive strength, and maximum load in the datasheet, and constrain both ends with set_output_delay / set_load. Then the repeater problem is theirs and is solvable.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
# What the integrator must be told, and what you must constrain.
# In your IP's SDC (shipped with the deliverable):
create_clock -name clk -period 4.000 [get_ports clk]
set_output_delay -max 1.200 -clock clk [get_ports {dout[*] dvalid}]
set_input_delay  -max 1.000 -clock clk [get_ports {din[*] dvalid_i}]
set_load 0.064 [get_ports dout[*]]     ;# what we assumed -- say it in the datasheet
# And in DATASHEET.md, the line that prevents the support call:
#   Output timing assumes <= 64 fF load and <= 150 ps transition at the port.
#   Beyond that, insert a repeater; every 100 fF adds approximately 25 ps.
여기서 틀린다. Repeaters are inverting. An odd number of them flips the polarity of the net, and the fix — using buffers (two inverters) instead — costs more delay than the inverter chain. Tools handle this, but hand-placed repeaters in a reset or scan-enable path are a classic bring-up bug: the block resets on the wrong polarity at one corner only, because that corner is the one where the inserted pair was optimised away.

T2.6 From cells to a path: what STA actually adds up

Static timing analysis does not simulate. It propagates two numbers through the graph — the earliest and latest time a signal can arrive — and compares them against what each endpoint requires. Everything above supplies one edge of that graph; this section is how the edges become a verdict.

유도 — Setup and hold, as one inequality each
이렇게 된다왜냐하면
1A launching flop puts data out at tlaunch = Tclk,launch + tcq.The clock arrives at the launching flop at its own time — clock delay is not the same at both ends of a path.
2It arrives at the capturing flop at tarrival = tlaunch + tlogic.tlogic is everything this chapter has been computing.
3The capture edge occurs at Tclk,capture + Tperiod, and the data must be stable tsetup before it.That stability requirement is a property of the flop, characterised the same way as any other arc.
4Setup: tarrival ≤ Tcapture + T − tsetup − tuncertainty. Slack is the difference.Clock uncertainty absorbs jitter and the skew the tool cannot yet know (pre-CTS). Positive slack passes; negative fails.
5Hold: the fastest path must not arrive before the same capture edge's hold window: tarrival,min ≥ Tcapture + thold.Hold has no T in it. Slowing the clock does not fix a hold violation — this is the single most useful fact in the chapter for a first-time designer.

Reading report_timing without being misled

A timing report is a ledger: clock path to the launch flop, cell and net delays along the data path, clock path to the capture flop, library setup, and the derates. The one line that decides whether the report is meaningful is the corner and mode it was run in — a report from the typical corner with ideal clocks is not evidence of anything.

어디에 쓰나Every timing signoff, every 'why is this failing' investigation, and every Fmax number you put in a datasheet.
언제 쓰나Read the whole path when slack is negative; read the startpoint and endpoint first when it is positive, to check that the tool timed the path you think it did. False paths and unconstrained endpoints show up here and nowhere else.
어떻게 쓰나Check: (1) is the endpoint a register, not a port; (2) is the clock real (propagated) or ideal; (3) what is the input transition at the worst cell — if it is at the end of the .lib index range, the number is extrapolated; (4) how much of the path is net delay. Above ~40 % net delay, the fix is placement, not logic.

실제로 난 일
This repository measured its own FIR filter at 455 MHz and then at 47.4 MHz — a factor of 9.6 — from the same RTL. The difference was a timing shell. Without registers at the boundary, the tool reported the delay of a path from a pin to a pin, which is not a path that exists in a chip. Every number in this chapter is only as meaningful as the path it was measured on, and the cheapest way to be wrong by 10× is to time the wrong path.

The next chapter takes the one case this one cannot handle: what happens when the setup inequality is violated not by a slow path but because the data was never synchronous with the clock at all. The answer is not a longer delay but a probability, and it is the only timing quantity in this book that can never be made zero.

특허로 가는 길 — 이 주제에서 발명이 일어나는 자리
The delay of a gate is physics and is not patentable. The way a block arranges itself so that a delay stops mattering is. Look at what the claims in shipping IP actually cover: a speculative (unrolled) decision-feedback equaliser that moves a feedback multiply-add off the critical loop and replaces it with a precomputed mux — the arithmetic is unchanged, the loop delay is not; a carry-select adder that buys delay with area by computing both carries; a clock-tree scheme that deliberately skews two launch points so that a slow path borrows time from a fast one. In every case the invention is where the delay was moved, not how fast a transistor is. That is why this chapter spends as much time on which term dominates as on the formulae: you cannot move a term you have not identified.
