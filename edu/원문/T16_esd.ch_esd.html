T16. ESD — the Failure That Happens Before the Chip Is Powered

Every pin must survive a few amps from a finger, and the circuit that survives it is in the signal path of the circuit that must not be slowed down by it.

A person walking across a carpet charges to a few kilovolts. When they touch a pin, that charge flows into the chip in a hundred nanoseconds, and every internal gate oxide is a few nanometres thick. Protection is therefore not optional and it is not a detail: it is a circuit in series with your signal, and its capacitance is subtracted directly from your bandwidth.

T16.1 Three models, three entirely different events

인체·기계·충전소자 모델 (HBM, MM, CDM)

HBM models a charged person: 1 pF charged to 2 kV, discharged through 1.5 kΩ. That gives a peak current of 1.33 A with a time constant of 15 ns and an energy of 2 µJ — computed, and worth doing once by hand because the numbers are less dramatic than people expect.

CDM models the chip itself charged and then discharged through one pin. It is far faster (rise time well under a nanosecond) and the peak current is several amps, but the total energy is small. Published CDM waveforms for 500 V give peaks of roughly 5–10 A — a figure from the standards and practice, not computed here, because the peak is set by the package's inductance rather than by a resistor.

MM (machine model) sits between them and is largely retired.

어디에 쓰나Qualification of every pin of every product. The targets are usually 2 kV HBM and 500 V CDM, and customers sometimes ask for more.
언제 쓰나At the very start of I/O design — the protection's capacitance is a term in the bandwidth budget and cannot be added later.
어떻게 쓰나Design for HBM at the pad with a clamp and diodes, and for CDM with secondary protection close to the gate it protects, plus a small series resistance. The two need different structures because they have different time scales.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
* TLP (transmission-line pulse) is how protection is characterised:
* rectangular 100 ns pulses of increasing current, measuring V at each step.
* The plot that comes out IS the design window check.
*   I_t2  : the current at which the structure fails (second breakdown)
*   V_h   : holding voltage after snapback  -- must be ABOVE the supply
*   V_t1  : trigger voltage                 -- must be BELOW the oxide limit
* A 2 kV HBM target needs I_t2 >= 1.33 A with margin -- the number computed above.
여기서 틀린다. HBM and CDM fail different structures. A design that passes 4 kV HBM can fail 250 V CDM, because CDM's current appears inside the die, rises in picoseconds, and finds the nearest thin oxide before the pad clamp has turned on. Reporting only HBM is reporting the easy half.

T16.2 The design window, and why it is a window

유도 — Four inequalities that must hold at once
이렇게 된다왜냐하면
1The clamp must not conduct in normal operation: Vtrigger > VDD,max including overshoot.A clamp that triggers on a supply transient is a short circuit across the power rail — the chip destroys itself while working.
2It must turn on before anything else breaks: Vtrigger < Voxide breakdown.The whole purpose. This and the previous line define the window, and in an advanced node with a thin oxide and a supply close to it, the window is only a volt or two wide.
3After snapback it must hold above the supply: Vhold > VDD.Otherwise, once triggered by an event, the clamp stays on when normal power returns — latch-up, and the part is destroyed on the bench after the ESD tester has already passed it.
4It must carry the current without failing: It2 > the target's peak, i.e. above 1.33 A for 2 kV HBM, with margin.Second breakdown is thermal and it is permanent.
The second and third inequalities move in opposite directions as the process scales: the oxide gets thinner (lowering the ceiling) while the supply falls more slowly (raising the floor). The design window narrows with every node, which is why ESD design has become a specialisation and why the protection for a new node is rarely a straight port of the old one.
전류 경로 (the current path) — the part people forget

Protection is not a component you attach to a pin; it is a path between every pair of pins. A pulse applied to an input with respect to some other I/O must find a low-impedance route: typically up through a diode to the local supply, along the supply bus, through a rail clamp to ground, and back through another diode.

Three things break that path and all three are layout, not schematic: bus resistance (the supply bus between the pin and the clamp — at several amps, a few hundred milliohms is volts), missing back-to-back diodes between separate supply domains, and a clamp that is too far away. The schematic shows the protection; only the extracted layout shows the path.

어디에 쓰나Every I/O, and especially every pin that sits in its own supply domain — a PHY's analog supply, a separately regulated PLL rail (T13).
언제 쓰나At floorplan, because the answer is 'a clamp near every pad' and that costs area at the edge of the die.
어떻게 쓰나Enumerate the pin pairs (I/O to I/O, I/O to any supply, supply to supply) and make sure each has a designed path. Then verify it on the extracted layout with an ESD-path checking tool — the voltage across the whole path at the target current is the number that matters, not the clamp's own voltage.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
# What an ESD path check reports, and how to read it.
#   pad_rx -> VSS  : clamp V 4.1 V + bus IR 1.3 V = 5.4 V  @ 1.33 A   <- FAIL (oxide 5.0)
#   pad_rx -> VDDA : diode 1.1 V + bus 0.4 V     = 1.5 V              <- ok
#   VDDA   -> VDD  : NO PATH                                          <- FAIL
# Two failures, and NEITHER is the clamp's fault:
#   the first is a BUS WIDTH problem; the second is a MISSING back-to-back
#   diode pair between two supply domains. Both are floorplan decisions.
여기서 틀린다. Separate supply domains are the classic hole. Two rails that are never connected on the die still get connected on the tester, and if no designed path exists between them the current finds one through whatever signal crosses the boundary — usually a level shifter's thin-oxide gate.

유도 — Why this table is the real ESD constraint for a SerDes
이렇게 된다왜냐하면
1A diode big enough to carry amps has a junction area, and a junction has capacitance.Protection capacitance and It2 are roughly proportional — you cannot have one without the other, which is the whole problem.
2That capacitance sits at the pad, in parallel with the line.At 300 fF and 25 Ω the pole is 21.22 GHz — below Nyquist for a 56 GBd link.
3So protection directly removes channel margin — the same margin T8's equalisers were fighting for.ESD and link budget are the same negotiation, and the ESD engineer is usually not in the room when the data rate is promised.
4The escapes are architectural: distribute the protection along an inductive network (T-coil) so the capacitance is absorbed into a transmission line, or use smaller primary protection plus fast secondary protection inside.Both are why this is a patent-dense area. Neither is free — a T-coil costs area and its own parasitics.
실제로 난 일
ESD is where a one-person IP house should be explicit about its limits. Designing and qualifying protection needs TLP measurement equipment and silicon to characterise, neither of which exists in a solo digital flow. The honest deliverable is therefore a soft macro that stops at the pad, an integration guide that states the assumed protection and its capacitance budget, and the sentence that this repository's rules demand: what was not verified, stated as not verified. A datasheet that implies ESD qualification that never happened is the kind of claim that ends a small vendor.

The computed numbers to carry: a 2 kV HBM event is 1.33 A for about 15 ns, carrying 2 µJ — small enough to be survivable and large enough to destroy a thin oxide. And protection of 300 fF at a pad puts a pole at 21.22 GHz, which is the sentence that connects this chapter to T8: ESD is not a separate discipline from the link budget; it is a line in it.

That closes the device half of this volume. It began with one device in four operating regions and ends at the pad, having passed through delay, metastability, noise, sampling, conversion, clocking, links, channels, switched capacitors, references, matching, power, ageing and geometry. The common thread was a single discipline: every number was computed or measured, the trivial explanations were killed before the number was reported, and what could not be established was said to be unestablished.

What follows is the other half: the flow that turns a verified netlist into manufactured, tested silicon. It is a different kind of engineering — the quantities are runtimes, track counts, corner lists, test seconds and defective parts per million rather than volts and picoseconds — but the discipline is the same one, and the six chapters apply it to the six stages: the flow and what a loop costs (T17), the floorplan and its power grid (T18), placement, clocking and routing (T19), signoff (T20), the verification environment that had to come first (T21), and the tester that every shipped part passes through (T22).

특허로 가는 길 — 이 주제에서 발명이 일어나는 자리
ESD is one of the most actively patented areas in I/O design, and the reason is the constraint itself: the protection sits in the signal path, so every claim is a way to get more protection per femtofarad. Distributed protection along a T-coil or an inductor-peaked network; active clamps that conduct only during a real event; protection that reuses the driver's own devices; and CDM-specific secondary protection placed at the gate rather than at the pad. If you ship a high-speed interface, this is the chapter where your differentiation lives.
