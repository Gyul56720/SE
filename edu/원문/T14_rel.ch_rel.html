T14. Reliability — What the Circuit Looks Like After Ten Years

Every parameter in this book drifts. A design signed off at time zero has been signed off for the wrong day.

A transistor that meets its specification on the day it is made will not meet it in ten years. Thresholds shift, oxides break down, and metal atoms move. None of this is a defect: it is the ordinary ageing of the materials, it is characterised and modelled by the foundry, and it is your job to budget for it — because a timing signoff at time zero is a signoff for a chip that has not yet been shipped.

T14.1 The four mechanisms, and what each one eats

표 17. The mechanisms you must budget for

Two of these are drifts and two are failures, and they need different arithmetic. A drift is budgeted as a margin: you sign off timing with a degraded library and accept the area cost. A failure is budgeted as a probability: you compute the fraction of parts that fail before the lifetime target and compare it against the allowed FIT rate. Mixing the two — for instance treating TDDB as 'a bit more Vth drift' — produces a design that meets its timing and fails its qualification.

T14.2 Acceleration: how a 1000-hour test predicts ten years

아레니우스 가속 (Arrhenius acceleration)

Wear-out rates follow exp(−Ea/kT), so raising the temperature multiplies the rate by a computable factor: AF = exp[(Ea/k)(1/Tuse − 1/Tstress)].

With Ea = 0.9 eV, stressing at 125 °C instead of 85 °C is 18.7× faster — which is how a 1000-hour oven test stands in for years of field use. The same arithmetic run backwards is the familiar rule of thumb: every 10 °C of cooling roughly 2.08× the life.

어디에 쓰나Qualification planning, and the argument with the thermal engineer about junction temperature.
언제 쓰나Whenever a lifetime is claimed. A lifetime without a temperature is not a specification.
어떻게 쓰나Pick Ea from the foundry's model for the mechanism in question — not a generic 0.7 eV — and state it alongside the result, because the answer depends on it strongly.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
# The same 1000-hour test, read with three different activation energies.
# This is why Ea must be quoted with any lifetime claim.
Ea = {0.7: 9.8, 0.9: 18.7, 1.1: 35.9}     # AF from 85 C to 125 C
# 1000 h at 125 C therefore stands for 9800 h / 18700 h / 35900 h at 85 C
#   -> 1.1 years / 2.1 years / 4.1 years.
# The SAME DATA supports "passes 1 year" or "passes 4 years" depending on a
# number the report often does not state.
여기서 틀린다. Activation energy is per mechanism, and using one number for a mixed failure population is the standard way to get a lifetime wrong by 3.68×. If a qualification report does not say which mechanism and which Ea, it has not made a claim you can check.

주어진 것. BTI's threshold shift grows as a power law in time, ΔVth ∝ tn, with n typically 0.15–0.25. A part is stressed for 1000 hours and shows some shift.
푸는 법. Ten years is 87,600 hours, or 87.6× the stress time. The shift grows by that ratio raised to n.
답. n = 0.15 → 1.96×; n = 0.2 → 2.45×; n = 0.25 → 3.06×. The same measurement predicts a shift that differs by 1.56× depending on an exponent nobody measured in this test.

여기서 틀린다. Extrapolating a power law from one time point. With a single stress duration, n is an assumption, not a measurement — and the answer is roughly linear in it. A credible BTI characterisation stresses at several durations and fits n; anything else is a number with an unstated multiplier.
Voltage acceleration is the reason a design must never be characterised only at nominal. At γ = 12 /V — a typical value — running 100 mV above nominal ages the device 3.32× faster, so a rail that overshoots during a DVFS transition can consume years of budget in seconds. The reliability question about DVFS is not the average voltage; it is the transients.

T14.3 Electromigration, and the rule that surprises digital designers

블랙 식 (Black's equation) and why AC is easier than DC

MTF = A·J−n·exp(Ea/kT), with n ≈ 2 for the usual regime. Lifetime falls as the square of current density and rises exponentially as temperature falls: at Ea = 0.9 eV, cooling a wire from 110 °C to 85 °C buys 6.7× its life.

The part that surprises digital designers: bidirectional (AC) current is far less damaging than unidirectional (DC), because atoms pushed one way are partly pushed back. EM rules therefore give a much higher limit for signal nets than for power nets — and the nets that fail are almost always power, clock drivers, or any signal with a duty cycle far from 50 %.

어디에 쓰나Power grid sizing, clock-tree driver outputs, and the output stage of any driver with a static current.
언제 쓰나Checked automatically by the EM/IR tools at signoff — but designed for long before, since the grid's width is a floorplan decision.
어떻게 쓰나Keep current density below the foundry's limit at the maximum junction temperature, and remember the limit depends on the duty cycle and direction. Vias are usually the binding constraint, not wires: one via carries far less than the wire it connects.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
# What the EM report actually flags, in order of frequency:
#   1. VIAS on power straps -- a single via where four were needed
#   2. Clock driver outputs -- high activity, high capacitance, one direction of
#      net current per edge
#   3. Long thin nets from a pad to a block at the far side of the die
# The fix for (1) and (2) is always the same: more parallel vias, wider metal,
# and it is FREE if done at floorplan time and expensive after routing.
여기서 틀린다. EM limits are specified at a temperature. A design checked at 85 °C and run at 110 °C has 6.7× less margin than the report says — and the junction temperature of a block just woken from sleep is not the average temperature anybody simulated.

T14.4 Designing for the tenth year, not the first

표 20. How each mechanism enters the design flow

Ageing mismatchσ(ΔVth) grows over life, so offsets and Vmin get worseAnalog designer — and it is often forgotten
실제로 난 일
The last row is the one that catches analog designs. BTI is not only a mean shift; it has a spread, and that spread adds to the mismatch of T12. A comparator whose offset was trimmed at test drifts back, because the two halves age differently — and they age differently precisely because they carry different duty cycles. A trimmed offset is trimmed for the day it was trimmed. Designs that must hold an offset for ten years either re-trim in the field (auto-zero, T10) or are built so that both halves age identically by construction.
노화 인지 설계 (ageing-aware design)

Three things make a design survive its tenth year, and none of them is margin alone:

  • Balance the stress. If a differential pair's two sides see different duty cycles, they will diverge. Swapping roles periodically (chopping, T10) makes them age together.
  • Prefer ratios of aged devices. A reference built from two devices that age identically drifts far less than one built from an absolute.
  • Allow recovery. BTI recovers partially when the bias is removed; a circuit that is DC-biased for ten years gets the worst case, and one that is duty-cycled does not.
어디에 쓰나Any IP with a ten-year or automotive lifetime target, and any analog block whose accuracy is trimmed.
언제 쓰나At topology selection. None of these can be retrofitted.
어떻게 쓰나Run the ageing simulation the PDK provides (a degraded model deck), with the real duty cycles — not with everything at DC — and check the offset and Vmin at end of life, not just the timing.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
* Ageing simulation: the same netlist, twice, with the PDK's aged models.
.lib 'models/pdk.lib' tt
.option reliability_analysis          $ vendor-specific; every PDK has one
.param age_years = 10
.param duty_cycle = 0.5               $ THIS is the parameter people get wrong --
                                      $ a DC-biased device is duty_cycle = 1.0
.op
.measure op vth_shift param='v(m1.vth_aged) - v(m1.vth_fresh)'
* Sign off BOTH decks. A design that only passes fresh is a design that only
* passes on the day it is made.
여기서 틀린다. Ageing models are statistical and conservative, and running them at the worst corner and worst duty cycle and worst temperature simultaneously produces a design that cannot be built. The skill is choosing which combinations are physically possible — a DC-biased device at maximum temperature is, but maximum activity at maximum temperature and minimum voltage usually is not.

The numbers to carry: at Ea = 0.9 eV a 125 °C stress runs 18.7× faster than 85 °C use, so 1000 hours stands for 2.14 년 — and with a different Ea the same data says 1.11 년 or 4.1 년. A lifetime claim without its Ea, its temperature and its duty cycle is not a claim. And 100 mV of overvoltage ages a device 3.32× faster, which is why the transients of a DVFS scheme matter more than its average.

The next chapter is where all of this becomes geometry: the layout, where a schematic that is correct in every respect can still fail.

특허로 가는 길 — 이 주제에서 발명이 일어나는 자리
Reliability physics belongs to the foundry; the IP-side claims are about surviving it: circuits that sense their own degradation and compensate (adaptive body bias, adaptive voltage scaling driven by on-die ageing monitors), topologies whose critical parameter is a ratio of two equally aged devices rather than an absolute, and duty-cycling schemes that let devices recover. The last one is worth noticing — BTI partially recovers when the stress is removed, which turns a physics constant into a design variable.
