Z12. Writing the Documents

Documentation is where a one-person house is at its biggest disadvantage and, unusually, where it can be at its best: a document written by the person who made the decisions is better than one written by a technical writer three months later. This part is the structure, the rules, and what this repository's own documents look like.

Z12.1 Three documents, three readers

Table 440. Who reads each one, and what they are deciding
DocumentReaderThe decision they are makingFails when
DatasheetAn evaluator, before buyingDoes this block do what we need, at the rate we need?Numbers without conditions; claims without evidence
Integration guideAn engineer, after buyingHow do I wire this in without calling them?It documents the block and not the integration
Known issuesBothCan I trust this supplier?It is absent, or it says ‘none’
The known-issue list is read as a character reference and should be written as one. An evaluator has seen datasheets before and knows every block has limitations; what varies is whether the supplier says so. A list with eight dated entries, each with impact, workaround and status, says that someone has thought about the failure modes and is willing to be held to them. An absent list says either that nobody looked or that somebody decided not to tell you, and the evaluator cannot tell which — so they assume the worse one. This is the cheapest credibility available to a supplier nobody has heard of, and it costs an afternoon.

Z12.2 The rules that make the numbers usable

Table 441. Six rules, each with the version that fails it
RuleGoodBadWhy
Every number carries its conditions‘2.18 cycles/byte, random back pressure, seeds 1/7/99’‘1 byte per cycle’A number without conditions cannot be reproduced or disputed
Name the tool, the library and the version‘208 cells, yosys 0.33, open library’‘~2 kGE’Part Y14 — the honest smaller claim beats the unattributed larger one
Say what was not measured‘Area in your process: not measured. Scripts included’SilenceSilence is read as a claim
Separate measured from derived‘measured 2.18; bound set at 2.6’‘2.6 cycles/byte’A bound and a measurement are different facts
Give the reproduction commandbash edu/house/release.sh crc32_stream—This is what turns claims into evidence
Date everything‘2026-09-19, v1.0’undatedAn undated known-issue list is a list of excuses
All six are about the reader's ability to check you, which is the only thing that distinguishes a datasheet from marketing. A vendor who makes checking easy is making a bet that the numbers hold, and evaluators read that bet correctly.

Worked example 73. How long the documents take if written weekly
Given. A 39-week block. Documentation written at the end, versus twenty minutes at the end of each week.
Method. Compare the totals, and then the quality, which is the part that does not appear in the total.
Written at the end1–2 weeks solid
Written weekly13 hours total
Ratioabout 3× cheaper
And the quality differenceweekly notes record why; end-of-project notes record what
What is lost by waitingthe reason a constant has its value, the corner that forced a structure, the thing that nearly went wrong
Who needs the whythe customer's integrator, and you in a year
How this is got wrong. By treating documentation as transcription. It is not — it is the record of decisions, and decisions are only recoverable while they are fresh. The three-times cost difference is real and is the smaller of the two effects; the larger one is that a document written at the end cannot contain the information that makes it worth reading. Part Z2 puts this in the Friday slot for exactly this reason, and it is the slot that disappears first.
Problem. Your block has 40 configuration parameters. How do you document them without writing 40 pages?
Solution. Generate the list and write prose only about the interactions, because the list is mechanical and the interactions are not. Generated: name, type, legal range, default, and what it affects — from the same source that produces the RTL parameters, exactly as Part Z4's register map is generated. That is a table nobody writes and nobody can get out of sync. Written by hand: the three or four combinations that matter — which parameters must move together, which ones are ignored when another is set, which ones change the area or the latency and by roughly how much. And a short list of the two or three configurations you have actually verified and synthesised, named, with their numbers. That last item is the honest core of it: 40 independent parameters is 240 configurations and you have verified a handful, so say which handful and state the covering argument for the rest (Part X19's pairwise criterion is the standard one). A customer choosing an unverified combination should know that is what they are doing, and a supplier who tells them is worth more than one who implies all 240 were tested.
