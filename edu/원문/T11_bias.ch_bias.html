T11. Bias and References — Making a Number That Does Not Move

Everything else in this book assumes a stable current or voltage exists. Making one costs more thought than using one.

A chip has no accurate absolute quantities. Resistors vary ±20 %, capacitors ±10 %, threshold voltages by tens of millivolts, and everything moves with temperature. Yet a data converter needs a reference stable to a fraction of an LSB, and an amplifier needs a bias current that does not double over the automotive temperature range. This chapter is about where that stability comes from — and the answer is that it is constructed by cancelling two opposite temperature dependencies against each other, which is why it is never free and never perfect.

T11.1 Two things a process gives you for free, both temperature-dependent

CTAT (complementary to absolute temperature) — A quantity that falls with temperature. The base-emitter voltage of a bipolar transistor at fixed current is the canonical one: VBE ≈ 0.7 V at room temperature with a slope of about −2 mV/°C, and it is not linear — the curvature it leaves behind is the floor on every plain bandgap.
PTAT (proportional to absolute temperature) — A quantity that rises proportionally with absolute temperature. The difference between two VBEs at a current-density ratio N is exactly PTAT: ΔVBE = (kT/q)·ln N. It is linear in T by construction, and it depends only on a ratio and on physical constants — not on process.
유도 — Why adding them in the right proportion gives ~1.2 V
이렇게 된다왜냐하면
1VBE(T) falls at about −2 mV/°C; ΔVBE = (kT/q)·ln N rises at +0.086·ln N mV/°C.For N = 8, that is +0.179 mV/°C — far too small on its own, which is why it must be amplified.
2Amplify the PTAT term by R1/R3 and add: Vref = VBE + (R1/R3)·ΔVBE.The gain is a resistor ratio — the one accurate thing a process offers (T10), which is why this topology survived.
3Choose the ratio so the slopes cancel at the temperature of interest.One equation, one unknown. The cancellation is exact only at one temperature because VBE is curved and the PTAT term is straight.
4The value at which the slopes cancel is the silicon bandgap extrapolated to 0 K — about 1.2 V. You do not choose it; it chooses itself.Measured below: the best temperature coefficient lands at Vref = 1.1652 V. Nobody designed that number — it is where the cancellation happens to occur.

여기서 틀린다. Assuming the optimum found in simulation is the optimum in silicon. The cancellation depends on VBE's actual slope, which is a process parameter with its own spread — so the optimum moves per lot. This is precisely why production references are trimmed at test, and why a design that cannot be trimmed must be designed for the worst-case optimum offset, not the nominal one.

Both tables tune the same physical quantity — the PTAT gain — and both show the same sharp optimum. The practical difference is cost: N enters as ln N, so doubling the area ratio buys only 0.69 of a unit of gain, while the resistor ratio is linear and free. That is why real designs use a modest N (8 is almost universal) and set the fine adjustment with resistors — the area would otherwise be enormous for a ratio that a resistor provides for nothing.
곡률 (curvature) — the floor the plain topology cannot cross

The measured best is 12.7  ppm/°C, and no choice of ratio does better. The reason is structural: VBE(T) contains a T·ln T term while the PTAT correction is exactly linear in T, so their difference cannot be zero everywhere. What remains is a bow — the reference is equal at two temperatures and off in between.

Curvature-corrected references add a third term with the right curvature (from a resistor's own temperature coefficient, from a PTAT² current, or from the difference of two VBEs at different current temperature dependencies) and reach single-digit ppm/°C. Each of those schemes costs area and a new sensitivity, which is why the plain topology is still shipped wherever tens of ppm are enough.

어디에 쓰나Any reference feeding a converter of 12 bits or more, and any system specified over the automotive or industrial temperature range.
언제 쓰나Compare the reference's drift with the converter's LSB over the operating range before choosing. At 12 bits and 1 V full scale an LSB is 244 µV; a 12.7  ppm/°C reference drifts 2,515 µV over 165 °C — about 10 LSB, which is why converters are specified at a temperature and references are trimmed.
어떻게 쓰나Measure TC as a box (max−min over the range, not a local slope), report the range it was measured over, and state whether it is trimmed. Those three facts make two references comparable; without them, nothing does.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
* Box TC, which is what a datasheet means, and the local slope,
* which is what a naive .measure gives.  They differ by 2-5x.
.dc TEMP -40 125 5
.measure dc vmax MAX v(out)
.measure dc vmin MIN v(out)
.measure dc vavg AVG v(out)
.measure dc tc_box param='(vmax-vmin)/vavg/165*1e6'   $ ppm/degC -- the honest one
* Reporting a slope taken at 27 C as 'the TC' is the standard way to make a
* reference look 3x better than it is.
여기서 틀린다. A temperature coefficient quoted without its range is not a specification. The box method over −40…125 °C and a local slope at 27 °C can differ by a factor of five on the same circuit, and the second number is the one that appears in optimistic datasheets.

T11.3 The failure mode that has nothing to do with temperature

시동 회로 (start-up) and the degenerate operating point

A bandgap's loop has two stable solutions: the intended one, and the one where every current is zero. Zero is perfectly self-consistent — no current means no VBE, which means no current — and nothing in the loop distinguishes it from the useful solution.

A start-up circuit injects a small current when the reference is near zero and then gets out of the way. Both halves matter: a start-up that keeps conducting adds its own temperature dependence to the output, and a start-up that is too weak leaves a fraction of parts dead on the slow corner.

어디에 쓰나Every bandgap, every constant-gm bias cell, every circuit whose bias is derived from itself.
언제 쓰나It is not optional and it is not a nominal-corner check: the zero state is reached by a slow supply ramp, by a brown-out, or by any sequence the designer did not think of.
어떻게 쓰나Simulate the supply ramp, not the operating point, at every corner and at several ramp rates including a very slow one. Then force the output to zero with an initial condition and verify that it recovers.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
* The two simulations that must both pass. The .op alone proves nothing --
* it simply finds A solution, and a degenerate circuit HAS a solution.
.tran 1u 500u                        $ 1) slow supply ramp
Vcc vcc 0 PWL(0 0 400u 1.8)          $   400 us ramp -- deliberately slow
.measure tran vref_final FIND v(ref) AT=480u

.ic v(ref)=0                         $ 2) forced into the dead state
.tran 1u 100u
.measure tran vref_rec FIND v(ref) AT=90u
* Both must land at the design value. A circuit that passes .op and fails these
* is a part that works on the bench and fails in the field at cold start.
여기서 틀린다. The most common start-up bug is not the absence of the circuit but its strength at the fast corner: a start-up device sized for the slow corner may still be injecting current at the fast one, shifting the reference by a few millivolts in a way that tracks process. Check that the start-up current is essentially zero once the loop is up — at every corner.

실제로 난 일
Load regulation is the specification that surprises digital integrators. A SAR converter's reference is not a DC load: it draws a charge pulse at every bit trial, and the reference must settle between trials to the full accuracy of the conversion (T10's settling argument, applied to a load that changes every cycle). A reference that meets its DC specification perfectly can still produce a converter with code-dependent INL, because the charge demanded depends on the code being tested. The fix is a local decoupling capacitor sized against the charge per trial — and that capacitor is often larger than the converter's own array.
일정 g_m 바이어스 (constant-g_m bias)

Most analog specifications — bandwidth, noise, gain — depend on gm, not on current. A bias that holds current constant therefore holds the wrong thing: gm = √(2μCox(W/L)I) still moves with mobility, which moves with temperature and process.

The constant-gm cell sets gm = 1/(R·(1−1/√K)) by forcing the source-degeneration resistor to define it — so gm now tracks a resistor rather than a mobility. It does not remove the dependence; it moves it to a component whose variation you can choose by picking a resistor type.

어디에 쓰나The bias of amplifiers, comparators, output drivers — anything whose bandwidth must be stable across corners.
언제 쓰나When bandwidth or noise must hold across process and temperature. It is the default for signal-path bias in mixed-signal IP.
어떻게 쓰나Pick the resistor type deliberately (poly resistors and diffusion resistors have very different temperature coefficients), and remember this cell also has a degenerate zero state — it needs its own start-up.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
* What the bias must actually hold, checked across corners.
* Constant CURRENT is the wrong target; constant gm is the right one.
.dc TEMP -40 125 5
.measure dc gm_min MIN @m.xamp.m1[gm]
.measure dc gm_max MAX @m.xamp.m1[gm]
.measure dc gm_spread param='(gm_max-gm_min)/gm_min*100'   $ percent
* A constant-current bias typically gives 30-40% here; constant-gm, under 10%.
여기서 틀린다. Constant-gm makes gm track 1/R, so the circuit's bandwidth now depends on the resistor's temperature coefficient — which for a diffusion resistor can be thousands of ppm/°C. Choosing the resistor is part of the design, not a layout detail.

The measured result to carry: the plain bandgap's best temperature coefficient in this simulation is 12.7  ppm/°C at Vref = 1.1652 V, and the optimum is sharp — a 2 % ratio error costs 2.91× the drift. Everything else in this chapter follows from that sharpness: it is why references are trimmed, why matching (the next chapter) is the limiting discipline, and why the value nobody chose — 1.2 V — is printed on every datasheet in the field.

특허로 가는 길 — 이 주제에서 발명이 일어나는 자리
References are an old field with a live patent stream, and it clusters in three places: curvature correction (every scheme that cancels the VBE non-linearity the plain bandgap leaves behind), low-voltage operation (current-mode and resistive-divider bandgaps that work below 1.2 V, which the classic topology cannot), and trim and calibration (one-point trim schemes that infer the curvature from a single measurement). The first and third are the same idea seen twice: accept a physical error and remove it somewhere cheaper — the pattern from T6.
