A precision analog circuit needs an accurate coefficient, and in an integrated process there is exactly one accurate thing: the ratio of two identically drawn structures. Absolute resistance varies ±20 % over process; absolute capacitance likewise; but the ratio of two capacitors drawn side by side matches to a fraction of a per cent. Switched-capacitor technique is the discipline of expressing everything you need in terms of that ratio — and paying for it with sampling, and therefore with time.

T10.1 A resistor made of a capacitor and a clock

유도 — From charge transfer to an equivalent resistance
이렇게 된다왜냐하면
1Charge a capacitor C to V₁, then dump it onto a node at V₂. The charge moved is ΔQ = C(V₁ − V₂).This is the whole circuit: two switches and one capacitor, driven by non-overlapping clocks.
2Repeat at fs. The average current is I = fs·C·(V₁ − V₂).Charge per cycle times cycles per second.
3That is Ohm's law with Req = 1/(fsC).For C = 1 pF and fs = 10 MHz, Req = 100 kΩ — a resistor that would be enormous in silicon, built from a capacitor that is not.
4A time constant made this way is ReqC₂ = C₂/(fsC₁) — a capacitor ratio divided by a clock frequency.Absolute values cancel. The accuracy of the circuit is now the accuracy of a ratio (fractions of a per cent) and of a crystal (parts per million), instead of the accuracy of an absolute RC (tens of per cent).

두 상 겹침 없는 클럭 (two-phase non-overlapping clocks)

The two switches must never be on together: if they are, the source and the destination are shorted and the transferred charge is wrong by an amount that depends on the overlap — which varies with process and temperature. The clocks are therefore generated with a deliberate dead time, typically from a cross-coupled NAND pair whose delay chains set the gap.

Bottom-plate sampling is the refinement that matters: open the switch connected to the fixed plate slightly first. That switch sees a constant voltage, so the charge it injects is constant and becomes an offset rather than signal-dependent distortion (T5's charge-injection problem, solved by sequencing instead of by circuitry).

어디에 쓰나Every switched-capacitor circuit: SC filters, SAR and pipeline ADC stages, sample-and-holds, reference buffers.
언제 쓰나Always. The dead time is not an optimisation — a design with overlapping phases fails at some corner, and it fails as a slow accuracy drift rather than as an obvious break.
어떻게 쓰나Generate the phases from one clock in a single cell, simulate the overlap at the fast corner (where it is smallest), and route the two phases together so they share delay variation.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
// Two-phase non-overlapping clock. The feedback is what guarantees
// the gap: neither output can rise until the other has fallen.
module clk_2ph (input clk, output logic p1, p1d, p2, p2d);
    wire n1 = ~(clk    & p2);          // cross-coupled NAND pair
    wire n2 = ~((~clk) & p1);
    assign p1 = delay_chain(n1);       // the chain sets the dead time
    assign p2 = delay_chain(n2);
    assign p1d = delay_small(p1);      // 'delayed' phases for BOTTOM-PLATE sampling:
    assign p2d = delay_small(p2);      // the fixed-plate switch opens FIRST
endmodule
// Verify at the FAST corner -- that is where the dead time is smallest and
// where overlap, if it exists at all, appears.
여기서 틀린다. Simulating the phase generator alone proves nothing: the overlap that matters is at the switches, after routing, with their real loads. A layout that routes one phase across the chip and the other locally has already broken the guarantee that the schematic provides.

T10.2 What the amplifier must do in half a clock period

유도 — Two requirements on one op-amp, and why they fight
이렇게 된다왜냐하면
1In the transfer phase the amplifier must settle the output to within ½ LSB.Anything less and the charge did not fully move — an error that is gain-like and therefore shows up as INL, not as noise.
2Settling is exponential, so reaching N-bit accuracy takes ln(2N) time constants.8.32 time constants for 12 bits, 11.1 for 16 — the requirement grows only logarithmically with resolution, which is the good news.
3The available time is half a clock period, so the closed-loop bandwidth must be at least ln(2N)/(π·Ts) — and the open-loop unity-gain frequency is that divided by the feedback factor β.β is the fraction of the output fed back, typically 1/2 to 1/4 in a gain stage; a small β multiplies the requirement.
4Separately, static accuracy needs loop gain: the residual error is 1/(Aβ), so A must exceed 2N+1/β.78.3 dB for 12 bits at β = 1, 102 dB for 16 — and this grows exponentially with resolution. This is the bad news, and it is why high-resolution SC design is hard in a process with low intrinsic gain.
5Bandwidth wants a short channel and lots of current; gain wants a long channel and cascodes, which cost headroom.The two requirements pull in opposite directions in every process node, and the gap has widened with scaling — which is why calibration and error-cancellation now appear in designs that would once have used a better amplifier.

Compare the two columns' growth. Going from 10 to 16 bits multiplies the settling requirement by 1.6 and the gain requirement by 64. Speed scales gently with resolution; static accuracy does not. That asymmetry is the reason the high-resolution end of the market moved to architectures that measure the amplifier's shortfall and correct it digitally, rather than architectures that demand a better amplifier.
상관 이중 표본화 (correlated double sampling)

Sample the amplifier's error — its offset and its low-frequency (flicker) noise — in one phase, then subtract that sample during the signal phase. Because the error is correlated between the two closely spaced samples, the subtraction removes most of it.

What it costs: the uncorrelated (thermal) noise of the two samples adds in power, so broadband noise gets worse by √2 while offset and flicker get much better. It also folds noise from above the sampling rate into the band (T5), so the amplifier's bandwidth must be limited deliberately.

어디에 쓰나Precision SC gain stages, image-sensor readout (where it is universal), any circuit whose input is near DC and whose amplifier has a flicker problem (T4).
언제 쓰나When offset and 1/f dominate — which is to say, when the signal band reaches below the flicker corner.
어떻게 쓰나Add one phase and one capacitor. Then check the noise budget again: the √2 penalty on thermal noise may cancel the benefit if the design was already thermal-limited.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
* The measurement that shows whether CDS helped -- run BOTH.
.noise v(out) VIN dec 20 1 100MEG      $ with CDS disabled
* ... then with CDS enabled, and compare the INTEGRATED noise over the signal band.
* Reporting only the 1/f improvement while the broadband floor rose by 3 dB is
* the kind of half-truth that gets found in the customer's lab.
여기서 틀린다. CDS removes what is correlated between the two samples. Noise at frequencies comparable to the sampling rate is not correlated and is not removed — it is aliased in. A design that samples the error long before it uses it has all of the cost and little of the benefit.

A switched-capacitor stage samples at least twice per cycle — once on the input capacitor and once on the feedback capacitor — so the noise power adds. The √2 is the minimum; a pipeline stage with several capacitors and a reference path can easily reach 2×. Taking the single-sample kT/C from T4 and sizing to it is a factor-of-two error in noise power, which is half a bit — and half a bit is exactly the kind of shortfall that gets discovered after tape-out.

The numbers to carry: reaching 16 bits needs 11.1 settling time constants — only 1.33× more than 12 bits — but 102.4 dB of DC gain against 78.27 dB, which is 16× more. Resolution is cheap in time and expensive in gain, and every modern architecture in this space is an answer to that one sentence.

특허로 가는 길 — 이 주제에서 발명이 일어나는 자리
The switched-capacitor field is old and its basic circuits are long out of patent, but two families keep producing claims: error-cancellation schemes (correlated double sampling, auto-zeroing, chopper-stabilised amplifiers, and every variant that measures an error in one phase and subtracts it in the next) and amplifier substitutes — ring amplifiers, dynamic amplifiers, comparator-based switched capacitor circuits — whose whole point is that a modern process cannot build the high-gain op-amp the textbook assumes. Both families answer the same question: what do you do when the op-amp is not good enough?
