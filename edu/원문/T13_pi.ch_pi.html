T13. Power Integrity — the Network Everything Else Sits On

The supply is not a node; it is an impedance with a frequency response, and your IP's worst jitter usually comes from its peak.

Every block in this book assumed a supply. This chapter is about what that supply actually is: a network of capacitors and inductances whose impedance varies by two orders of magnitude across frequency, with peaks at frequencies nobody designed. The current your IP draws flows through that impedance and becomes a voltage on your own supply — and the failures it causes appear as jitter (T7), reference drift (T11), or timing failures that correlate with software.

T13.1 The specification is an impedance, not a voltage

목표 임피던스 (target impedance)

Ztarget = VDD·(allowed ripple)/Imax. For 1 V, 5 % ripple and 10 A of transient current that is 5 mΩ — and it must hold at every frequency the load actually contains, not on average.

This turns a vague requirement ('clean supply') into a curve that can be measured, simulated and signed off, which is the whole reason the concept exists.

어디에 쓰나Board design, package design, and the on-die grid — and it is the number your IP's integration guide should state as a requirement.
언제 쓰나Before the package is chosen. The package's inductance sets the mid-frequency impedance and cannot be fixed later by board capacitors.
어떻게 쓰나Take the block's maximum transient current and its spectral content, set the allowed ripple from the most sensitive circuit (usually a PLL or a reference, not the logic), and check the impedance curve against it at the peaks.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
# What an IP deliverable should say, so the integrator can meet it.
# INTEGRATION.md:
#   Supply: 1.0 V +/- 5%, Z_pdn <= 5 mOhm from DC to 200 MHz at the block's pins.
#   Peak transient: 10 A over 2 ns at the start of a burst (di/dt = 5 A/ns).
#   On-die decoupling included in the macro: 20 nF.
#   The PLL supply (VDDA) requires a separate regulator; sharing it with the
#   digital rail has been measured to add 0.4 ps rms jitter at 1 GHz.
# Without these four lines the integrator guesses, and the guess is usually wrong
# in the direction that makes your IP look bad.
여기서 틀린다. A flat target is a simplification. The real requirement is the load's current spectrum times the impedance, integrated where the victim circuit is sensitive — a 20 mΩ peak at 1.4 MHz matters enormously to a PLL whose loop bandwidth is 1 MHz (T7) and not at all to a logic block.

T13.2 Anti-resonance: why adding a capacitor can make it worse

유도 — Where the peak comes from
이렇게 된다왜냐하면
1A real capacitor is series R–L–C: capacitive below its self-resonance, inductive above it.ESL comes from the package and the mounting; it is why a 100 µF bulk capacitor does nothing at 100 MHz.
2Put two capacitors in parallel. Between their self-resonances, one is already inductive and the other is still capacitive.An inductor in parallel with a capacitor is a parallel resonant tank, and a parallel tank has high impedance at resonance.
3So the combination has a peak where the individual parts each had a dip.Measured on this PDN: peaks at 1.4 MHz (21.66 mΩ) and 11.1 MHz (6.683 mΩ).
4The peak's height is set by the damping — the ESR in the loop.Which produces the result below, and it is the one that surprises people.

Read that table twice. Going from 5 mΩ to 100 mΩ of ESR — making each capacitor worse by the usual figure of merit — lowers the anti-resonance peak from 26.77 mΩ to 7.018 mΩ, a factor of 3.81. Past the optimum it rises again, because the branch stops conducting at all. 'Lowest ESR' is not the specification; the right ESR is. This is why controlled-ESR capacitors exist and why a well-meaning substitution to a 'better' part can break a board that was working.

T13.3 The three droops, and which one is yours

표 15. A current step produces three separate droops

di/dt, and why the derivative is the enemy

The resistive drop I·R is easy to compute and rarely the problem. The inductive drop is L·di/dt, and di/dt in a modern digital block is enormous: a clock-gated domain waking up can move amperes in nanoseconds.

Worse, di/dt is periodic and correlated with the workload. A processor running a loop that alternates between a heavy and a light instruction produces a current square wave at whatever rate the loop runs — which can land exactly on an anti-resonance. The classic symptom: a machine that fails one specific benchmark and passes everything else.

어디에 쓰나Any design with clock gating, power gating, or a bursty accelerator — which is every SoC.
언제 쓰나At architecture time. Staggering the wake-up of a wide datapath is a micro-architectural decision, not a physical one.
어떻게 쓰나Stagger enables over several cycles, ramp clock-gate release, and never release a whole power domain on one edge. On the analysis side, drive the PDN model with the real current profile, not a step.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
// The cheapest di/dt fix in RTL: stagger the ungating.
// Releasing 64 lanes on one edge is a current step; releasing them over 8 cycles
// is the same charge with 1/8 the di/dt, and usually nobody notices the latency.
always_ff @(posedge clk) begin
    if (wake) stagger <= {stagger[6:0], 1'b1};     // 8-deep shift
    else      stagger <= '0;
end
generate for (genvar i = 0; i < 64; i++)
    assign lane_en[i] = stagger[i/8];
endgenerate
// Measured effect belongs in the integration guide: peak di/dt with and without.
여기서 틀린다. Simulating the PDN with a current step gives the worst case and is useful, but it hides resonance: a step contains all frequencies at declining amplitude, while the real failure is a periodic load that concentrates energy at one frequency. Drive the model with the real workload's current spectrum as well.
실제로 난 일
Supply noise reaches your IP through two doors, and only one of them is the supply. The first is direct: VDD moves, and a VCO's frequency moves with it (T7's pushing). The second is the substrate: current returning through the die's bulk modulates body voltages, which modulates thresholds (T1's body effect). The second path does not appear in any PDN impedance plot, and it is why physical separation and guard rings matter even when the supply is separately regulated.

특허로 가는 길 — 이 주제에서 발명이 일어나는 자리
Power delivery is a board and package discipline, but the IP-side claims are real and they all have the same shape: make the block insensitive instead of making the supply clean. Regulators local to a sensitive block, differential and supply-referenced signalling, bias schemes whose output does not track VDD, and clock distribution that is immune to supply modulation. Every one of them is cheaper than lowering a PDN's impedance by the same factor.
