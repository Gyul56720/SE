Z13. Week One

This book is long and the first Monday is short. This part is what to do in the first five days if the aim is a two-member team producing sellable IP, in an order that leaves something working at the end of each one. Everything it names exists in this repository and can be copied.

No architecture, no algorithm, no big block. The week builds the apparatus; the apparatus is what makes every later week cheap. A week spent the other way round produces a nicer block and no way to know whether it works.
The instinct is to start with the interesting block. It is the reason to be doing this at all, and it is the wrong first week: a large block with no gate is a large block you cannot make claims about, and the gate is far easier to build against something trivial. The 30-line GF multiplier in this repository exists for exactly that reason — it is small enough that every part of the apparatus can be seen working, and the apparatus transfers unchanged to a block a hundred times larger.

How this is got wrong. By counting the week as lost time. It is the only week whose output is reusable without modification across every block the house will ever build, and the ratio in the fifth row means it pays for itself the first time the gate catches something. The honest caution is that it does not feel like progress, which is why it is worth deciding in advance to spend it.

Problem. You have read this book and you have a day job. Is any of this possible part-time?
Solution. The apparatus is, and the block is not — and separating the two is the useful answer rather than a discouraging one. The apparatus is compatible with part-time work because the agent runs when you are not there: an hour in the evening spent strengthening the oracle is repaid by a night of unattended checking, which is the one part of this arrangement that does not scale with your hours. Week one is five evenings rather than five days. What is not compatible is the customer side: evaluations, plugfests, support response times and the conversations of Part Z5 all happen during business hours and cannot be deferred, and Part Z8's arithmetic says the customer side is what decides whether the business exists. So the honest part-time plan is to build the apparatus and one small, complete, publishable block — which is a portfolio (Part Y13) and an asset and a genuine thing to point at — and to treat the transition to selling as a separate decision that needs the day job to end. Conflating the two is how people spend two years part-time and arrive with neither.
