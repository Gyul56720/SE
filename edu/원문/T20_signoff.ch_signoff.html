T20. Signoff — The Checks That Decide Whether It May Be Built

Every signoff check answers one narrow question, and the failures that reach silicon live in the gaps between them.

A routed design is a set of polygons and a claim: that these polygons, made in this process, will behave the way the RTL said. Signoff is the set of independent checks that test that claim before anyone spends mask money on it. Each check is a different tool with a different model of the design, and it is worth being precise about which question each one answers — because the failures that reach silicon are almost never inside a check; they are in the space between two.

T20.1 What each check actually asserts

표 48. The signoff set, stated as claims
CheckThe claim it establishesWhat it cannot see
DRCThe geometry obeys every manufacturing rule the foundry publishes for this process and this metal stackWhether the geometry implements the design. A DRC-clean empty die passes
LVSThe devices and connections extracted from the layout match the netlist, device for device and net for netWhether the netlist is right. LVS compares the layout to the schematic; if the schematic is wrong, LVS confirms the error
ERCNo floating gates, no unconnected wells, no supply shorted through a device that was not meant to conductAnything dynamic — it is a topology check
Antenna (T19)No metal segment collects enough charge during etch to damage a gate oxideNothing about the finished circuit
Density / fillEvery layer's local metal density is inside the window CMP requiresThe capacitance the fill adds — that is extraction's job, and it must be re-run after fill
EM / IRNo wire exceeds its current-density limit for the required lifetime (T14), and no node sags below the budgetWhether the switching activity used to drive it resembles the real workload — see T20.3
STAEvery timing path meets setup and hold, at every corner in the list, in every mode in the listPaths the SDC excluded, and modes nobody listed
Read the third column as a list of where to look after a silicon failure. Each check is sound; each is narrow; and the narrowness is deliberate, because a check that tried to establish everything could not be run in finite time. The engineering judgement in signoff is not in running the checks — it is in knowing what the union of them leaves out.
금속 채움 (metal fill) and the extraction it invalidates

Chemical-mechanical polishing removes material at a rate that depends on the local pattern density. A region with little metal polishes faster and ends up thinner, which changes both resistance and the thickness of the dielectric above it. The foundry therefore requires every layer's density to sit inside a window, and the flow satisfies that by adding fill — floating or grounded metal shapes in the empty areas.

Fill is added after routing, which means it is added after the extraction that timing signoff used. Those shapes are metal next to signal wires; they add capacitance. Grounded fill adds it predictably; floating fill adds a smaller, harder-to-model amount.

So the order is: route, fill, re-extract, re-run STA. A flow that signs off timing on the pre-fill extraction has signed off a design that will not be manufactured.

어디에 쓰나Between detail routing and final timing signoff.
언제 쓰나Always. The only variation is whether the fill is inserted by the P&R tool or by the physical-verification tool, and the two do not produce identical results.
어떻게 쓰나Insert fill, re-extract with fill present, re-run the corner set, and diff the slack against the pre-fill run. A small, uniform degradation is expected; a large change on a few nets means fill landed next to a long, critical, sparsely-surrounded wire.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
# The diff that must exist before tape-out:
#   path                   slack pre-fill   slack post-fill   delta
#   u_dsp/…/mac_out[17]        +45 ps           +31 ps        -14 ps
#   u_if/…/clk_gate_en          +88 ps           +12 ps        -76 ps   <- look
# A -76 ps outlier is a wire that was alone in an empty region and is now
# surrounded by fill. It is real, it is predictable, and it is missed by any
# flow that signs off before filling.
여기서 틀린다. Fill also changes the inductance and the return path of high-speed signals, which matters for the links of T8 far more than for logic. For those, ask for fill exclusion zones — and remember that an exclusion zone is a density violation unless the surrounding area compensates.

T20.2 Corners multiply, and most of them are dominated

유도 — Where the signoff runtime comes from
이렇게 된다왜냐하면
1Process: 3 · Voltage: 2 · Temperature: 3 · RC (interconnect): 3 · Mode: 4.Each axis is independent of the others — that is why they multiply rather than add. The counts are assumed and typical.
2That is 3 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 4 = 216 corner-mode combinations.Multiplication. Nothing subtle is happening; that is the point.
3At 2  h per STA run, a full set is 432  h of compute.18  days on one machine — which is why signoff runs on a farm and why the corner list is negotiated rather than assumed.
4A single ECO invalidates all of it.This is T17's loop-cost arithmetic in its most expensive form: the last stage in the chain is the largest one.
표 49. The axes, and why each one is there
AxisValuesWhy it cannot be dropped
Process3slow / typical / fast — and slow-n/fast-p corners on top of that in a mixed-signal part
Voltage2nominal and the low end of the regulator's tolerance
Temperature3−40 °C, 25 °C, 125 °C
RC (interconnect)3cmin / cworst / rcworst — extraction corners are independent of the device corners
Mode4functional, scan-shift, scan-capture, low-power retention
MMMC — analysing modes and corners together instead of one at a time

The 216 combinations above are not 216 separate analyses in a modern flow. Multi-mode multi-corner analysis loads the several mode constraints and the several corner libraries into one session and propagates once, sharing the work that is common between them.

The saving is real but it is not the main reason to use it. The main reason is that optimisation must see all the corners at once. Fixing a setup violation at the slow corner by adding drive strength can create a hold violation at the fast corner; a tool that sees one corner at a time will oscillate between them, and that oscillation is exactly the non-convergence of T17 with p ≥ 1.

어디에 쓰나Post-CTS optimisation onwards, and all of signoff.
언제 쓰나From the first optimisation that can make a corner-dependent trade — which in practice means as soon as hold fixing starts.
어떻게 쓰나Define the analysis views explicitly (a view is one mode plus one delay corner plus one RC corner), mark which are for setup and which for hold, and check that the list covers the product's real operating envelope rather than the list inherited from the previous project.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
# An analysis-view list is a specification, not a configuration:
#   view              mode        delay corner   rc corner   checks
#   func_ss_cw        functional  ss_0.9v_125c   cworst      setup
#   func_ff_cb        functional  ff_1.1v_m40c   cbest       hold
#   shift_ss_cw       scan_shift  ss_0.9v_125c   cworst      setup
#   shift_ff_cb       scan_shift  ff_1.1v_m40c   cbest       hold
# The question to ask of this table is not "is it right" but
# "which operating condition of the product is NOT in it".
여기서 틀린다. Scan-shift mode is the one most often forgotten and the one most often broken, because its clock structure is different from the functional one and its hold requirements are severe. A part that fails only on the tester is usually a shift-mode timing problem, not a defect.

The corner list is long partly because the dominance people expect does not hold. The intuition is that the slow corner bounds setup and the fast corner bounds hold, so two corners would do. That intuition comes from a regime where delay increases monotonically with temperature — and modern low-voltage operation is not in that regime.

유도 — Temperature inversion, from the device model of T1
이렇게 된다왜냐하면
1Delay ∝ C·V / Id, with Id ∝ µ(T)·(V − Vth(T))α.The α-power form from T1. α ≈ 1.3 for a short-channel device.
2Mobility falls with temperature: µ ∝ T−1.5. That makes the part slower when hot.Phonon scattering. This is the effect everyone knows.
3Threshold also falls with temperature: dVth/dT ≈ −0.8  mV/K. That makes the part faster when hot.The Fermi level moves with temperature. This is the effect that gets forgotten.
4Which one wins depends on the overdrive V − Vth, because the threshold shift is a large fraction of a small overdrive and a small fraction of a large one.The two effects enter multiplicatively, so the comparison is between their logarithmic derivatives.
5d(ln delay)/dT = 1.5/T − ακ/(V − Vth). Setting it to zero gives V − Vth = ακT/1.5.Differentiate and solve. The result depends on nothing but the two temperature coefficients and α.
6At 300  K that is an overdrive of 0.208  V, i.e. V ≈ 0.658  V.Below that supply the part is slowest when cold, and the corner list must contain both temperature extremes for setup.

The table stops at 0.6  V for a reason worth stating: at −40 °C this model's threshold is 0.503  V, so below about 0.5  V of supply the device has no overdrive at all when cold and the delay is not merely long, it is undefined. That is not an artefact — it is the near-threshold regime, and it is why a low-voltage retention mode has a minimum supply specified at the cold corner.
The independent control. The crossover supply is computed twice: once from the closed form V = Vth0 + ακT/1.5, giving 0.658  V, and once by bisecting on the sign of a numerically differentiated delay, using none of that algebra, giving 0.658  V — a disagreement of 2.53×10-8  mV. And the trivial explanation is killed by the table rather than argued away: if the effect were an artefact of the normalisation, the 'inverted' verdict would not move with supply, and it does.
실제로 난 일
A design signed off at 125 °C and typical voltage failed at −40 °C in a cold-start test. It was a low-voltage retention mode: at that supply the part was slowest cold, and the corner list — inherited from a 1.8 V project where the intuition held — contained only the hot corner for setup. Nothing in the flow was broken. The list of what to check was written by someone applying a rule that had stopped being true.

T20.3 EM and IR signoff need something the design does not contain

정적 IR (static IR) · 동적 IR (dynamic IR) · 스위칭 벡터 — Static IR analysis distributes an average current over the grid and solves the resistive network — the calculation of T18. Dynamic IR analysis solves the grid with the supply's inductance and decoupling included (T13), driven by the current waveform that a particular pattern of switching produces. That pattern is the switching vector, and it is not part of the design: it has to be supplied.
This is the one signoff check whose answer depends on an input that is neither in the netlist nor in the constraints. Choose a quiet vector and the analysis passes; choose the worst one and it may not — and nothing in the flow tells you which you chose. The honest procedure is to state, in the signoff report, which vectors were used and why they are believed to bound the real workload, and to treat a dynamic-IR pass without that statement as an unverified claim rather than a result.
표 51. Three ways to get a vector, and what each is worth
SourceHow representativeWhat it misses
Functional simulation of a real workloadBest available — it is the actual switchingOnly covers the workload you simulated, and long simulations produce enormous activity files
Vectorless (tool-propagated probabilities from a few toggle rates)Adequate for a first pass and for finding gross grid errorsCorrelation. It assumes blocks switch independently, and the failures come from everything switching at once
Scan-capture patternThe worst case for many designs and rarely analysedNothing — it is often the real answer, because scan capture switches far more of the die simultaneously than any functional mode does
예제 8. Which mode sets the peak current?
주어진 것. The block of T18/T19 draws 350 mW functionally. In scan-shift mode every flop toggles on every cycle, and the shift clock runs at a quarter of the functional frequency.
푸는 법. Functional switching activity for logic is typically 10–20 % of nodes per cycle; shift activity approaches 50 % of flops per cycle. Take the clock network as fixed (it toggles either way) and compare the flop-output switching: roughly 0.15 → 0.5 is a factor of about 3.3 in that component, against a factor of 0.25 from the slower clock.
답. Shift mode draws roughly 0.8× the functional switching power in that component — comparable, not obviously smaller. The peak is set by whichever mode runs its clock fastest at high activity, and on many parts that is scan capture, not shift.
This is also why test engineers reduce shift frequency and use low-power ATPG that limits simultaneous toggling — not for test quality, but because the part cannot supply the current. That constraint belongs in the floorplan discussion of T18, and it usually arrives after it.
여기서 틀린다. The numbers above are ratios of assumptions, not measurements, and should not be quoted as results. The conclusion that survives is structural: test modes are not low-power modes, and a grid signed off only against functional vectors has not been signed off against the mode that will run first, on the tester, on every part shipped.

T20.4 Changing a signed-off design

표 52. Two kinds of ECO, and what each one costs
KindWhat changesWhich signoff must be redone
Metal-only ECO (uses spare cells)Routing only. The function comes from cells already placed and already on the mask, rewired on the metal layersEverything downstream of routing: extraction, STA, EM/IR, DRC, LVS. But only the changed masks are re-made — which is the entire point
Functional ECO (new cells)Placement and routing. New instances go into gaps, displacing what was thereAll of the above, and every mask is new
스페어 셀 (spare cells) — paying now for the option to change later

Spare cells are unused gates — typically a mix of NANDs, NORs, inverters and flops — scattered through the placement and tied off. They cost area and leakage and do nothing. What they buy is the ability to implement a logic change by rewiring metal only, because the transistors the change needs are already on the diffusion masks.

The economics are a straightforward option price. A full mask set at an advanced node costs millions; a metal-only revision costs a fraction of it and takes weeks rather than months. Spending 1–2 % of the area on spares is cheap insurance if the probability of needing a change is anything but negligible — and on a first silicon it never is.

어디에 쓰나Inserted at placement, distributed rather than clustered.
언제 쓰나Every design that will have a first silicon. Omit them only on a well-understood derivative of a proven part.
어떻게 쓰나Scatter them so that any region has some within reach — a spare cell 500 µm from the fix is not usable, because the wire that reaches it breaks the timing the fix was made to satisfy. Include flops: the changes you cannot predict are usually sequential.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
# A spare-cell plan is a distribution, not a count:
#   region      spares: inv  nand2  nor2  dff     nearest-spare distance
#   core NW           24     16     16     8            < 60 um
#   core NE           24     16     16     8            < 60 um
#   near macro A       6      4      4     2            < 90 um   <- thin
# 'We have 400 spare cells' says nothing. 'No point in the core is more
# than 90 um from a spare flop' is the statement that makes an ECO possible.
여기서 틀린다. Tie the spare inputs off properly and check that ERC sees them as tied. An untied spare gate is a floating input — the failure mode is excess current through a half-on inverter, and it passes DRC, LVS and STA because none of them is looking for it.
테이프아웃 (tape-out) — The point at which the GDS is released to the mask shop. It is a commercial event rather than a technical one: after it, changing the design costs a mask set. Everything this chapter describes exists to make the claim that the release is safe — and the honest form of that claim always includes the list of what was not checked.

T20.5 What to do with this on Monday

기능ECO · 금속ECO · 스페어셀Placement, months before you need themWhether a post-silicon fix costs a metal revision or a full mask set

The numbers to carry: 216 corner-mode combinations at 2  h each is 18  days of compute that one ECO throws away — the strongest quantitative argument in this book for fixing things early. And the crossover overdrive of 0.208  V is why "slow is hot" stopped being a safe rule: below about 0.66  V of supply, in this model, the slow corner is the cold one.

The part can now be built. Whether an individual die works is a different question again, answered not by a tool but by a machine with the die in it — and that is the last chapter of this arc.

특허로 가는 길 — 이 주제에서 발명이 일어나는 자리
Signoff itself is the least patent-encumbered part of the flow — the rules belong to the foundry and the checks are commodity. What is filed heavily is ECO automation: choosing which spare cells to use and how to reroute with the minimum disturbance, so that a fix does not invalidate the signoff that has already been paid for. That is a direct attack on the cost computed in T17.
