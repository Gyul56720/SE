Worked example 71. What the agent can check that a human review cannot
Given. RS(544,514) over GF(2^10), t=15. The decoder must correct every pattern of up to 15 symbol errors and must not silently miscorrect beyond.
Method. Count the patterns. Then note which of the two properties is checkable by sampling and which is not.
Error positions of weight 15C(544,15) ≈ 1029
Magnitudes per position1,023
Total patternsastronomically many
What sampling establishesa bound on the failure rate, not correctness
What the algebra guaranteesall patterns of weight ≤ t, by the code's minimum distance
So the RTL must be checked againstthe model, not the space
And the model againstthe algebra — which tests/test_rs.py does by property, not by sample
How this is got wrong. By trying to verify the RTL directly against the specification's guarantee. The guarantee is over a space nobody can enumerate; what is checkable is agreement with a model whose own correctness rests on properties that are checkable. This two-level structure is why the golden model must be verified by properties rather than by vectors — and it is exactly what this repository's test_rs.py does when it breaks the syndrome computation and asserts the measurement notices.

Parameterising is not a substitute for reading the clause, and the plan should not pretend otherwise. A parameterised decoder is correct for whatever parameters it is given and interoperable with nothing until the parameters are right. What parameterising buys is that the unknown stays visible — a hard-coded constant dissolves into an assumption nobody questions, and Part Y15's scorecard would quietly lose its most important row. The first item in the plan remains: obtain the document. Everything else is work that can proceed in parallel, and none of it is work that can substitute.
Problem. Sixteen weeks of RTL and the specification is still unobtainable. Do you build it anyway?
Solution. Build the parts that are parameter-independent and stop before the parts that are not, and be explicit with yourself about which is which. The field arithmetic, the Berlekamp–Massey iteration, the Chien search structure and the Forney computation are all generic — they are correct for any primitive polynomial and any first root, and building them is not speculative. The top-level packing, the interoperability claim and the datasheet are parameter-dependent and should not be written. What you must not do is pick a plausible value and proceed as though it were confirmed, because that converts a known unknown into an unknown one, and the failure surfaces at a plugfest in front of the people you most want to impress. The strategic answer, though, is to reconsider the product. Part Z5's scorecard weights specification availability at 3 precisely so that this situation is visible before sixteen weeks are spent, and its winner — a post-quantum core whose standard, reference implementation and test vectors are all public — exists because that row was weighted honestly. A block you cannot obtain the specification for is not a cheaper project; it is a different one.
