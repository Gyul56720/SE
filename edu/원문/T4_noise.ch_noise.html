T4. Noise — and Why It Sets the Size of Everything

Noise is the one specification you cannot improve with cleverness alone. Every 6 dB costs 4× the capacitance, and therefore 4× the power.

Everything measured in a circuit arrives with noise on it, and the noise does not come from poor engineering — it comes from the thermal motion of charge and from the discreteness of the electron. That makes it the hardest kind of specification: you cannot remove it, you can only spend area and power to average it down, and the exchange rate is fixed by physics at 4× the cost per extra bit.

This chapter establishes the three noise sources you will actually meet, then derives the one number that sizes half the analog blocks in a mixed-signal IP (kT/C), and then does something unusual for a noise chapter: it crosses into the digital side, where the same word means something entirely different and where the consequence — the voltage below which an SRAM cannot be read — is measured here with ngspice rather than asserted.

유도 — Why the answer always ends up as kT/C
이렇게 된다왜냐하면
1A resistor's thermal noise density is 4kTR, independent of frequency.Nyquist's result from equilibrium thermodynamics — it contains no device parameter beyond R, which is why it is a floor and not a design choice.
2Put that resistor in series with a capacitor C — which is what every sampling switch is.The switch's on-resistance (T1: triode region) and the sampling capacitor are in series; there is no third element.
3The RC low-pass shapes it, and the noise bandwidth of a single pole is π/2 × f−3dB = 1/(4RC).Integrating 1/(1+(f/f0)²) over all frequency gives π/2·f0, not f0 — a factor people drop and then wonder why measurements are 1.6× worse than predicted.
4vn² = 4kTR × 1/(4RC) = kT/C.R cancels. The noise on a sampled capacitor does not depend on the switch at all — only on C and temperature.
5For C = 1 pF at 3 K, vn = 64.4 µV rms.One number worth memorising; everything else scales as 1/√C from here.
kT/C — the rule that sizes capacitors, and therefore power

A sampled node carries kT/C of noise power regardless of how it was sampled. To keep that below the quantisation noise of an N-bit converter (LSB/√12), the capacitor must satisfy C ≥ 12kT·22N/VFS². Every extra bit quadruples C, and because settling in a fixed time needs gm ∝ C, it quadruples the current too.

This single relation is why a 16-bit converter is not "a bit harder" than a 12-bit one: it is 256× the capacitance.

어디에 쓰나Every switched-capacitor circuit: SAR and pipeline ADC sampling networks, sample-and-holds, switched-capacitor filters and gain stages, the reference buffer that has to drive them.
언제 쓰나At the very start of an analog block's design, before any transistor is sized. The capacitor comes first because it fixes the power budget.
어떻게 쓰나Fix VFS and N, compute C, then size the driver for settling within the sampling phase (T2's RC arithmetic with the switch's Ron from T1), then check that the reference can supply the charge each cycle.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
* The measurement that separates kT/C from everything else you did wrong.
* Transient noise in ngspice: sample the same node 1000 times and take the std dev.
.tran 1n 10u
.noise v(out) VIN dec 20 1 1G      $ small-signal, for the spectrum
.measure noise inoise_total_rms param='sqrt(inoise_total)'
* And the closed form to check it against -- ALWAYS have the hand calculation:
*   v_n = sqrt(kT/C) = sqrt(1.38e-23*300/1e-12) = 64.4 uV for C = 1 pF
여기서 틀린다. kT/C applies to the sampled value. During the tracking phase the noise bandwidth is set by the circuit, not by the sampling — so a track-and-hold that is measured while tracking shows a different (usually larger) number than the same circuit measured after the hold. Reporting one when the spec means the other is a common and expensive mistake.

T4.2 Flicker noise, and the corner that decides your architecture

플리커코너 (flicker corner, f_c) — The frequency at which the 1/f density equals the thermal density. Below it the circuit is flicker-dominated, above it thermal-dominated. For a modern CMOS amplifier it lands anywhere from ~100 kHz to a few MHz for NMOS inputs, typically a decade lower for PMOS.
Where the signal sits relative to the corner

Flicker noise density rises as 1/f, so its integrated power over a band from f1 to f2 is proportional to ln(f2/f1) — it depends on the ratio, not the width. A DC-coupled measurement has f1 → 0 and the integral diverges logarithmically: the longer you average, the worse it gets, which is the opposite of thermal noise.

That single fact drives an architectural decision. If the signal is near DC (a temperature sensor, a bandgap reference, a DC offset), averaging does not help and you must move the signal — chop it up to a frequency above the corner, amplify there, and bring it back down. If the signal is already above the corner (an RF or high-speed link front end), flicker is irrelevant and you spend nothing on it.

어디에 쓰나Sensor front ends, reference and bias circuits, comparator offset, any DC-accurate measurement. Also the phase noise of an oscillator, where device flicker is up-converted into close-in phase noise.
언제 쓰나Whenever the band of interest includes frequencies below ~1 MHz. Ask the question explicitly at architecture time; retrofitting chopping into a finished amplifier is a redesign.
어떻게 쓰나Reduce it by area (S ∝ 1/WL — 4× the area for 6 dB), by choosing PMOS inputs, or by modulation: chopping (square-wave modulate in and out; leaves residual ripple at the chop frequency) or correlated double sampling (sample the offset, subtract it; costs noise folding and bandwidth).
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
* Flicker in the model card is one parameter -- and simulators differ.
.model NM NMOS (LEVEL=54 ... KF=1e-25 AF=1.0 EF=1.0)
* KF alone means nothing: the density is KF/(Cox*W*L*f^EF), so a KF taken from
* another PDK, or from a model with a different EF convention, is a WRONG number
* that still simulates.  Always sanity-check the corner against measured silicon:
.noise v(out) VIN dec 10 1 100MEG
* then read the point where the 1/f asymptote crosses the flat part.
여기서 틀린다. Chopping does not reduce noise; it moves the signal away from the noise. The thermal floor is unchanged and the chopper adds its own switching artefacts at the chop frequency and its harmonics. A design that reports a beautiful low-frequency noise figure and a spur at fchop has not improved anything if the spur lands in band.

T4.3 The other noise: static noise margin, measured

On the digital side the word 'noise' means something different and the quantity is not a spectral density but a voltage: how much DC disturbance a storage node can absorb before it flips. The structure is the same cross-coupled pair that produced metastability in T3, viewed statically instead of dynamically.

정적잡음여유 (SNM, static noise margin) — The largest DC voltage that can be inserted in series with both storage nodes of a cross-coupled pair without destroying its bistability. Geometrically it is the side of the largest square that fits inside the butterfly curve formed by the two inverter transfer curves.
실제로 난 일
The extractor used below was wrong twice before it was right, and both times the numbers looked plausible. The first version measured a horizontal gap instead of an inscribed square and reported hold SNM ≈ 0.5·VDD — too high. The second version had the access transistor wired with its gate on the bitline instead of the word line; it produced read SNM that fell as the cell ratio rose, which is backwards. The trend caught the bug, not the value. That is the rule this repository runs on: before believing a measurement, check the direction it moves when you change something you understand.
The extractor was then self-checked against two limits: an inverter with very high gain must give SNM → VDD/2 (it gives 0.4825) and an 'inverter' with gain 1 must give 0 (it gives 0.0). Only after both did the cell numbers below get written down.

유도 — Why reading a cell is what almost destroys it
이렇게 된다왜냐하면
1To read, the word line turns on both access transistors while both bit lines are precharged high.That is the only way to sense the cell without a separate read port.
2The access transistor on the '0' side now pulls that node up, through a divider formed with the cell's own pull-down NMOS.Two NMOS devices in series between VDD and ground, with the storage node in the middle — the node cannot stay at 0 V.
3The '0' node rises to roughly VDD·(1/(1+β)) in the resistive limit, so a β of 2 already puts it a third of the way up.Both devices are in triode at that point; the ratio of their strengths is the divider ratio. This is the only reason the cell ratio exists as a design parameter.
4That rise eats directly into the margin: measured read SNM at β = 2 is 0.226 V against a hold SNM of 0.414 V — 45.3 % of the margin is gone the moment the word line rises.Measured, not asserted — the deck and the extractor are in this chapter's source.
5Raising β raises read SNM and slightly lowers hold SNM.Measured: hold falls from 0.421 V to 0.409 V across the same sweep, because a stronger pull-down skews each inverter's trip point away from VDD/2. There is no free direction, which is what makes it a design point rather than a rule.
V_min — the voltage at which the memory, not the logic, stops the chip

Read SNM does not degrade gracefully with supply. Measured on the same cell at β = 2, it holds near 0.22 V from 1.0 V down to 0.7 V and then collapses: 0.145 V at 0.6 V and 0.087 V at 0.5 V. The knee sits where VDD approaches 2Vth (0.7 V here) — below that the devices are in weak inversion, the divider ratio depends on exponential currents, and mismatch (T12) turns a marginal cell into a failing one.

This is why the SRAM sets the chip's minimum operating voltage, and why DVFS schemes either stop above the array's Vmin or give the array its own supply.

어디에 쓰나Any SoC with on-chip SRAM and more than one voltage point: the whole DVFS range, retention voltage in sleep modes, and the low-voltage corner in signoff.
언제 쓰나As soon as a power mode is proposed. The question 'can we drop to 0.6 V' is an SRAM question before it is a logic question.
어떻게 쓰나Either raise Vmin with assist circuits (word-line underdrive for read, negative bit line for write), split the array's supply, or use an 8T cell where the read path does not touch the storage node.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
* The sweep that produces the number above -- run it at every corner,
* not just typical, because Vmin is a MISMATCH-limited quantity (see T12).
.param beta=2.0
.dc VIN 0 VDD 0.004 VDD 1.0 0.5 -0.1     $ nested sweep: input, then supply
.print dc v(vr)
* Extraction is NOT a .measure one-liner: you need the butterfly and the largest
* inscribed square.  Script it, and SELF-CHECK the extractor on two limits
* (very high gain -> VDD/2, gain 1 -> 0) before trusting any cell number.
여기서 틀린다. SNM measured at the typical corner is nearly useless for Vmin. The array fails at its worst cell out of millions, so the quantity that matters is the tail: Vmin is set by roughly the −5σ to −6σ cell, which means the analysis is a Monte Carlo with importance sampling, not a nominal DC sweep. The sweep above tells you the shape; it does not tell you the yield.

T4.4 From volts to a datasheet number: SNR, SNDR, ENOB

표 84. The three numbers, and what each one hides
QuantityDefinitionHides
SNRSignal power / noise power, distortion excludedDistortion. A converter with terrible linearity can have excellent SNR
SNDR (SINAD)Signal / (noise + all distortion)Which of the two dominates — always report the spectrum too
ENOB(SNDRdB − 1.76) / 6.02That it is derived, not measured. ENOB is SNDR wearing different units; an ENOB quoted without its input frequency and amplitude is not a measurement
SFDRSignal / largest single spurBroadband noise entirely — a clean-looking SFDR with a poor noise floor is common
예제 13. How many bits does 64 µV of noise allow?
주어진 것. A 1 V full-scale converter with C = 1 pF on its sampling network, so the sampled noise is 64.4 µV rms, and assume everything else is perfect.
푸는 법. A full-scale sine has rms amplitude VFS/(2√2). SNR = 20·log10(signal rms / noise rms), then ENOB = (SNR − 1.76)/6.02.
답. SNR = 74.8 dB, giving ENOB = 12.1 bits. A 1 pF sampling capacitor is a ~12-bit part, no matter how good the rest of the design is.
여기서 틀린다. Forgetting that the sampling network is usually sampled twice (input and reference), and that a differential pair of capacitors doubles the noise power while doubling the signal amplitude — the two partly cancel, and the factor you get depends on the topology. Write the specific network down; do not reuse a remembered factor.

Two measured results to carry out of this chapter. A 1 pF sampling capacitor is a 12.1-bit part — the capacitor decides the resolution before any transistor is drawn. And reading a 6T SRAM cell costs 45.3 % of its static margin at a cell ratio of 2, which is why that cell ratio exists and why the array, not the logic, sets the lowest voltage the chip can run at.

The next chapter takes the sampling operation that this one treated as instantaneous and gives it a finite aperture, a jitter, and a spectrum — which is where the noise floor of a high-speed converter actually comes from.

특허로 가는 길 — 이 주제에서 발명이 일어나는 자리
Noise is thermodynamics; nobody patents kT. What gets claimed is every arrangement that buys back the 4× — correlated double sampling, chopping, auto-zeroing, noise shaping, and the family of techniques that let a circuit spend its noise budget only where the signal is. On the digital side the same shape appears as assist circuits for SRAM: negative bitline, word-line underdrive, read-modify-write assist — all of them arrangements that let a cell keep its margin at a voltage where the plain cell has none. Read this chapter asking: where am I paying the 4× rule, and what would let me not pay it?
