T12. Matching — Two Identical Devices Are Not Identical

The only accurate thing on a chip is a ratio, and even that is a distribution. Everything precise is built on knowing its width.

Two transistors drawn identically, adjacent, in the same orientation, still differ. Dopant atoms are discrete and land at random; the gate edge is not perfectly straight; the oxide thickness varies atom by atom. The resulting parameter difference is a random variable, and precision analog design is the discipline of knowing its standard deviation and budgeting for its tail.

T12.1 Pelgrom's law, and why it is a square root

펠그롬 법칙 (Pelgrom's law)

For two adjacent identically drawn devices, the standard deviation of a parameter difference falls as the square root of area: σ(ΔVth) = AVT/√(W·L), with AVT in mV·µm — a per-process constant that the foundry measures and publishes (a few mV·µm, improving slowly with each node).

The square root is not empirical curve-fitting: the mismatch comes from counting statistics on independent microscopic events (dopant placement, edge roughness), and the relative fluctuation of a count of N independent events falls as 1/√N. Area is the count.

어디에 쓰나Every current mirror, differential pair, comparator, capacitor array, and SRAM cell in the design. Also in the yield model — this is the distribution that Vmin (T4) is a tail of.
언제 쓰나Before drawing anything precise: the required σ fixes the minimum area, and the minimum area often fixes the whole block's size and its parasitic capacitance, and therefore its speed.
어떻게 쓰나Work backwards from the specification. If an offset of 5 mV at 3σ is allowed, then σ(ΔVth) ≤ 1.67 mV, so W·L ≥ (AVT/1.67 mV)². With AVT = 5 mV·µm that is 8.96 µm² — per device.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
* Mismatch in a PDK is a MODEL PARAMETER, not something you add by hand.
* The foundry supplies statistical models; you enable them and run Monte Carlo:
.lib 'models/pdk.lib' mc              $ NOT the 'tt' corner -- the statistical deck
.mc 200 op                            $ 200 samples
.measure op ios param='abs(i(vd2)-i(vd1))'
* AND THEN: the corners and the mismatch are DIFFERENT analyses.
*   corner  = global shift  (all devices move together)
*   mismatch = local spread (devices move independently)
* Running 'tt + mismatch' and calling it worst case misses the combination that
* actually fails: slow corner AND an unlucky pair.
여기서 틀린다. AVT applies to the difference between two adjacent devices. Using it for the absolute value of one device, or for devices on opposite sides of the die, is wrong in both directions: the absolute spread is larger (it includes global process variation) and the device-to-device correlation across a die is a different model entirely.

유도 — Reading Pelgrom straight out of that table
이렇게 된다왜냐하면
1Area 0.2 µm² → σ(ratio) = 14.16 %; area 0.8 µm² → 7.17 %.Area ×4, σ ÷1.97 — the square-root law, measured, not assumed.
2The next two steps repeat it: ÷1.99 and ÷1.99.Three independent confirmations of the same exponent. If a measurement like this does not give √, the model or the harness is wrong — and that is the fastest check available on a mismatch setup.
3The mean ratio is not 1: it rises from 0.9779 toward 0.9966 as the devices grow.A symmetric Vth error maps to an asymmetric current error — the square-law is convex, so the average of the ratio is pulled below 1. Mismatch biases the mean, not only the spread, and the effect vanishes only as the spread does.

유도 — Why more current matches better, and what it costs
이렇게 된다왜냐하면
1A Vth error δ produces a current error through the transconductance: δI = gm·δ.The device does not know why its gate voltage is effectively different.
2Relative current mismatch is therefore σ(I)/I = (gm/ID)·σ(ΔVth).gm/ID is the whole story (T1). High gm per amp — the efficient, low-power operating point — is also the worst-matching one.
3In strong inversion gm/ID = 2/Vov, so σ(I)/I = 2σ(ΔVth)/Vov.Measured: 22.1 % at 1 µA against 2.29 % at 100 µA — 9.65× better for 100× the current, which is the √ again (Vov ∝ √I).
4In weak inversion gm/ID saturates at ~1/(nVT) ≈ 25–30 V⁻¹ and matching is at its worst.This is why a subthreshold current mirror is a bad current mirror, and why ultra-low-power analog pays for its efficiency in area.
Those two tables are the whole design space. Accuracy can be bought with area (σ ∝ 1/√WL) or with overdrive (σ ∝ 1/Vov), and both have a square-root character, so both are expensive. Everything else in precision analog — chopping, auto-zero, DEM, digital calibration — exists to avoid paying either.

T12.3 The mismatch that is not random

계통 불일치 (systematic mismatch) — A difference that is the same on every die: a gradient in oxide thickness across the wafer, a temperature gradient from a nearby power device, stress from the package or from a neighbouring well, or simply different surroundings on the two sides of a pair. It does not average out over samples, so no amount of Monte Carlo finds it.

공통 중심 배치 (common-centroid layout) and dummies

Split each device of a matched pair into pieces and interleave them so both devices share the same centre of mass: A B B A, or a checkerboard for a two-dimensional gradient. A linear gradient then affects both halves equally and cancels to first order.

Dummy devices at the edges solve a different problem: the outermost real device has a different neighbour than the inner ones, so etch and stress differ. Dummies give every real device identical surroundings, at the cost of area that does nothing electrically.

어디에 쓰나Every matched pair that matters: differential pairs, current-mirror arrays, capacitor arrays in converters (T6), bandgap BJT arrays (T11).
언제 쓰나At layout, and it must be specified by the designer — a layout engineer cannot infer which devices must match from a netlist.
어떻게 쓰나Mark matched groups in the schematic, require common-centroid with dummies, keep orientation identical (never mirror a device to save area), route the two halves symmetrically, and keep the pair away from the package's stress maxima — typically the die corners.
실제 코드는 이렇게 생겼다
// The instruction that must reach the layout engineer. A netlist
// does not carry it, so it goes in the constraint file AND the schematic.
//   match_group  : M1 M2      common_centroid = ABBA, dummies = 2 each side
//   orientation  : identical (NO mirroring -- STI stress is not symmetric)
//   routing      : symmetric; equal parasitic on both gates
//   keep_out     : > 50 um from the die seal ring and from any power device
// Verified by an LVS-with-matching check, not by inspection:
//   assert centroid(M1) == centroid(M2) within 0.5 um
여기서 틀린다. Common centroid cancels a linear gradient. A quadratic one — which is what a nearby heat source produces — is not cancelled, and interleaving can even make it worse by spreading the device over more of the curvature. For thermal gradients the answer is distance and orientation relative to the isotherms, not interleaving.
실제로 난 일
Mismatch is also the reason SRAM Vmin (T4) is a yield question rather than a design-point question. An array of 10 million cells needs the worst cell to work, which is roughly a −5.5σ event. Ordinary Monte Carlo with a thousand samples cannot see that tail at all — it would take on the order of 108 samples. Production flows therefore use importance sampling or analytic tail methods, and a report that says 'Monte Carlo passed, 1000 samples' for a memory has not addressed the question it was asked.

The measured results to carry: current-mirror mismatch falls as the square root of area — 14.16 % → 7.17 % → 3.61 % → 1.81 % for four doublings of area — and as the square root of current: 22.1 % at 1 µA against 2.29 % at 100 µA. Both knobs cost a square, and that single fact is why calibration exists.

The next chapter leaves the devices and takes up the network that feeds them all — the power delivery network — where the same 'it is a distribution' argument reappears as a question about impedance and time.

특허로 가는 길 — 이 주제에서 발명이 일어나는 자리
Matching itself cannot be patented — it is statistics — but every scheme that buys accuracy without area can be: dynamic element matching, chopper and auto-zero offset cancellation, digital foreground and background calibration, and layout techniques that cancel gradients. Notice the shape again: since σ falls only as 1/√area, buying 4× accuracy with area costs 16× the silicon — so almost every commercially interesting idea in precision analog is an alternative to paying that.
